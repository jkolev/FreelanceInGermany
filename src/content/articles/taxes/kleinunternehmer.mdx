<ShortAnswer>

The small business exemption (<Term de="Kleinunternehmerregelung" />) lets you invoice without charging VAT and skip VAT returns. You qualify if your net revenue (before VAT) last year was €25,000 or less and your revenue this year stays at or under €100,000. In the year you start, the limit is €25,000. The trade-off is that you cannot reclaim the VAT you pay on business purchases, and the payment that takes you over a limit is already taxed.

</ShortAnswer>

## Who can use it?

The rule is in § 19 of the VAT law (UStG). VAT (<Term de="Umsatzsteuer" />) is the sales tax added to most invoices. You qualify when your business is based in Germany and both of these are true:

- Your revenue in the previous calendar year was no more than €25,000.
- Your revenue in the current calendar year has not gone over €100,000.

These limits have applied since 1 January 2025 and have not changed since. Older articles quote €22,000 and €50,000, which were the limits until the end of 2024. Each year's figures are collected on [freelancer numbers](/freelancer-numbers).

The exemption works for both freelancers (<Term de="Freiberufler" />) and trade businesses (<Term de="Gewerbe" />). [Freiberufler vs. Gewerbe](/start/freiberufler-vs-gewerbe) explains the difference.

If you start your business during a year, there is no previous year to test. In that first year, only the €25,000 limit applies, not €100,000. If you stay within €25,000, you keep the exemption in your second year, as long as that year stays at or under €100,000.

## What counts as revenue?

The limits use your revenue, not your profit. Specifically:

- Amounts are net, which means before VAT.
- Your costs are not subtracted.
- A payment counts in the year you receive it, not the year you send the invoice. An invoice sent in December and paid in January counts toward January.
- All your self-employed work counts together, however many clients you have.
- Selling business equipment, such as an old laptop, does not count.

Keep a running total of the payments you receive, in a spreadsheet or in an accounting app such as <AffiliateLink slug="accountable">Accountable</AffiliateLink>. It is the easiest way to see a limit coming.{/* LINK: once published, also link "accounting app" to /taxes/accounting-software */}

## What changes when you use it?

Three things change. You do not add VAT to your invoices. You do not file VAT returns, unless the tax office (<Term de="Finanzamt" />) asks for one. And you cannot reclaim the VAT you pay on business purchases (<Term de="Vorsteuer" />). Income tax is not affected; see [taxes for freelancers](/taxes/freelancer-taxes) for how it works.

The alternative is regular VAT (<Term de="Regelbesteuerung" />). You charge VAT, file returns and reclaim the VAT on your purchases. [VAT returns explained](/taxes/vat-returns) covers the filing side.

<ComparisonTable>

| | Small business exemption | Regular VAT |
|---|---|---|
| VAT on invoices | None | You add it, usually 19 percent |
| VAT returns | None, unless the tax office asks | Monthly or quarterly in most cases, plus an annual return |
| VAT on business purchases | You pay it and cannot reclaim it | You can reclaim it |
| Revenue limits | €25,000 last year and €100,000 this year | None |
| Main downside | No VAT refund on purchases, and the limits can end it | More paperwork, and VAT raises your price for private clients |
| Best for | Low revenue, few purchases, mostly private clients | Mostly business clients, or large purchases that carry VAT |

</ComparisonTable>

## Who does each option tend to suit?

The exemption tends to suit people with low revenue, few expenses and mostly private clients. A private client cannot reclaim VAT, so adding 19 percent makes your price 19 percent higher for them.

Regular VAT tends to suit people who work mainly for businesses, or who expect large purchases. A business client that can reclaim VAT is usually no worse off when you charge it.

Take a laptop that costs €2,000 plus €380 VAT. With regular VAT, you get the €380 back through your VAT return. With the exemption, you bear it as a cost.

Opting out is hard to undo (see below), so it can be worth asking a [tax advisor](/taxes/tax-advisor) (<Term de="Steuerberater" />) to run your numbers first. An advisor can also tell you whether a separate VAT exemption under § 4 UStG, such as for medical or teaching work, applies to you instead.

## What goes on your invoices?

- No VAT amount and no VAT rate. If you show VAT anyway, you may have to pay it to the tax office, and your client cannot reclaim it.
- A note that you use the exemption. Everyday wording is enough if it is clear, such as *steuerfreier Kleinunternehmer*. Many people also cite § 19 UStG.
- An ordinary PDF or paper invoice is always allowed. You do not have to issue an e-invoice, which is a structured digital format.

[How to write a German invoice](/taxes/invoice) lists the other required details and has a template.

## How do you start using it?

You do not apply. The exemption applies by law when you meet the limits. When you register as self-employed, the <Term de="Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung" /> asks for your expected revenue. It is also where you can choose to opt out.{/* LINK: once published, add: "[The questionnaire in English](/start/tax-registration-questionnaire) walks through it." */}

If you registered earlier and are unsure which option you chose, check the questionnaire you submitted or ask the tax office.

## What happens if you go over a limit?

The two limits work differently.

If you pass €25,000 in a year after your first year, you keep the exemption for the rest of that year. From 1 January of the next year, you charge VAT, even if your revenue drops again.

If you pass €100,000 in a year, or €25,000 in your first year, the exemption ends at once. Payments you received before that point stay VAT-free. Payments you receive afterward are taxed, even for work you did earlier, and the normal VAT filing duties apply.

<Callout title="The whole payment is taxed">

The payment that takes you over the limit is subject to VAT in full, not only the part above the limit. Check your running total before you invoice a large job.

</Callout>

## Can you choose regular VAT instead?

Yes. You declare it to the tax office, and no particular form is required. You can do it in the registration questionnaire or later by message through <Term de="ELSTER" />.

The declaration applies from the start of the year you choose. The deadline is the end of February two years after that year. For 2026, that means the end of February 2028.

<Callout title="Opting out is hard to undo">

You cannot withdraw the declaration. It binds you for at least five calendar years. After that, you can revoke it from the start of a later calendar year.

</Callout>

## What about clients in other countries?

Only sales that are taxable in Germany count toward the limits (§ 19 Abs. 2 UStG). Services to business clients abroad are usually taxed where the client is based (§ 3a UStG), so they generally do not count. Services to private clients abroad are often taxed in Germany and then do count. Cross-border rules have exceptions, so ask a tax advisor if much of your work is international.

Germany also lets small businesses use the small business exemptions of other EU countries (§ 19a UStG). This needs a separate registration with the Federal Central Tax Office (<Term de="Bundeszentralamt für Steuern" />), and your revenue across the whole EU must stay at or under €100,000. Rules differ by country, so check each country's rules before relying on it.
