<ShortAnswer>
To become a freelancer in Germany, you register your address, make sure your residence permit allows self-employment, and send an online tax registration form to your tax office (<Term de="Finanzamt" />) within one month of starting work. If your work counts as a trade (<Term de="Gewerbe" />) instead of a liberal profession (<Term de="Freiberufler" />), you also register with the local trade office when you start. You need health insurance either way. If you are not a citizen of an EU or EEA country or Switzerland, you need a permit that allows self-employment before you start working.
</ShortAnswer>

## What are the steps, in order?

Most people follow these nine steps. Your situation may let you skip some.

1. Register your address (<Term de="Anmeldung" />).
2. Check that your residence permit allows self-employment, if you are not an EU, EEA or Swiss citizen.
3. Create your account in <Term de="ELSTER" />, the tax office's online portal.
4. Work out whether your work counts as Freiberufler or Gewerbe.
5. Register your trade, but only if it is a Gewerbe.
6. Send the <Term de="Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung" /> (tax registration questionnaire).
7. Arrange health insurance.
8. Decide how to handle VAT, then set up invoices, a bank account and bookkeeping.
9. Look at pension and other insurance.

Two of these steps depend on letters in the post. One is your personal tax ID, which arrives after you register your address. The other is your ELSTER activation code. These letters are usually the slowest part, so steps 1 and 3 are the ones to start first.

## How do you register your address?

You must register within two weeks of moving into a home in Germany. You do it at your local registration office, often called the <Term de="Bürgeramt" />. Bring your passport or ID and a written confirmation from your landlord (<Term de="Wohnungsgeberbestätigung" />), and check whether your city requires an appointment.

This step matters for freelancing because everything that follows needs a German address. After you register, the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt) also assigns you a personal tax ID (<Term de="Steuer-ID" />) and mails it to you. You keep that number for life. After a first registration in Germany, the letter can take four to eight weeks. If nothing has arrived after three months, you can request it from the BZSt.

If you are already registered at your address, you can skip this step.

## Can you work for yourself with your residence status?

Citizens of EU and EEA countries and Switzerland do not need a residence permit to freelance in Germany.

Everyone else needs a permit that allows self-employment. The Residence Act has two routes. One is for people who start a business, and it asks whether there is an economic interest or regional demand for your work. The other is for freelancers in liberal professions. It does not apply that test, but you must show you can fund your plans and hold any license the work requires.

A permit for self-employment is usually first issued for up to three years. It can be extended if your income covers your living costs.

If you already hold a permit for another purpose, such as a job, study or family, it may carry a note on whether self-employment is allowed. If that is not clear, ask the foreigners' authority (<Term de="Ausländerbehörde" />) before you start.{/* LINK: once published, add: "The article on the [freelance visa](/start/freelance-visa) covers the requirements in detail." */}

<Callout title="Permit first">

Working for yourself without a permit that allows it can have serious consequences for your residence status. The permit has to be in place before you start, not after.

</Callout>

## Are you a Freiberufler or a Gewerbe?

German law separates two kinds of self-employed work, and the difference decides which offices you deal with.

A Freiberufler works in a liberal profession. The tax code describes this as scientific, artistic, writing, teaching or educational work. It also lists professions such as doctors, lawyers, engineers, architects, translators and journalists.

Any other independent work done for profit is a Gewerbe, or trade. Some jobs sit in a grey area, and the tax office decides which category yours falls into after you describe your work in the registration questionnaire.

What changes in practice:

- A Freiberufler registers no trade, pays no trade tax and joins no chamber of commerce.
- A Gewerbe is registered with the trade office. It pays trade tax (<Term de="Gewerbesteuer" />) once profit passes a yearly allowance, and it generally belongs to a chamber, usually the local chamber of commerce (<Term de="Industrie- und Handelskammer" />, IHK).

Both groups pay income tax. Some regulated professions also need their own license or chamber membership, for example doctors, lawyers and architects. The article on [Freiberufler vs. Gewerbe](/start/freiberufler-vs-gewerbe) explains how the tax office decides.

## How do you register a trade?

You only do this step if your work is a Gewerbe. Register at your city's trade office (<Term de="Gewerbeamt" />). In some cities another local office handles it, so check your city's website. The trade law says to register at the same time you start, not afterward. The office charges a fee that varies by city.

Some trades need a permit before you begin. The registration does not replace it.

The trade office passes your details to the tax office. Do not wait for a form to arrive by post, though. You are expected to send the tax questionnaire yourself within one month.

## How do you register with the tax office?

Every freelancer must tell the tax office when they start work, whether Freiberufler or Gewerbe. You do this with the tax registration questionnaire. Since 2021 it normally has to be sent electronically through ELSTER.

To use ELSTER, you register an account with your Steuer-ID. ELSTER emails you an activation ID and mails you an activation code, which usually arrives within about two weeks. If you have a German ID card with the online function switched on, you can verify your identity online instead and skip the letter. If you already have an ELSTER account from filing taxes as an employee, you can use it.

If your Steuer-ID has not arrived yet, ELSTER also lets you register with only an email address. That account can send the tax registration questionnaire and nothing else, and you upgrade it once your tax number arrives.

The questionnaire asks for your personal details, a description of your work, your start date, and the sales and profit you expect. It also asks whether you want the small business VAT rule and whether you need a VAT ID. Both are explained below. The tax office can use your profit estimate to set your income tax prepayments, so a realistic number matters.

Once the tax office has processed the form, it issues your business tax number (<Term de="Steuernummer" />). This is not the same as the Steuer-ID. The Steuer-ID is personal and permanent. The Steuernummer belongs to your file at a particular tax office, and it goes on your invoices. Processing times vary by office.

<Callout title="One-month deadline">

The law gives you one month from the day you start work to inform the tax office. The Steuer-ID letter and the ELSTER activation letter can take weeks between them, so a late start on step 1 or step 3 can use up that month. If your Steuer-ID is the problem, use the email-only ELSTER registration to send the questionnaire on time. If even that is not possible, the tax office can accept a paper form in cases of hardship. That is the office's decision, so contact it before relying on it.

</Callout>

The article on [the tax registration questionnaire in English](/start/tax-registration-questionnaire) goes through the form field by field.

## Do you need health insurance as a freelancer?

Yes. Everyone who lives in Germany must have health insurance, and this has applied since 2009. As an employee, your employer paid part of the cost. As a freelancer, you pay all of it yourself.

There are two systems: public (statutory) insurance and private insurance. Which one is open to you, and what it costs, depends on your history. People leaving a job, people arriving from abroad and people who were previously private insured all face different rules. If you are leaving a job, there are short deadlines for deciding what happens to your public cover.

The [complete guide to health insurance for freelancers](/health-insurance/freelancers) explains each case, and [public vs. private](/health-insurance/public-vs-private) compares the two systems. Whether an expat or travel policy counts depends on your situation.

The article on [what public health insurance costs freelancers](/health-insurance/public-cost) shows the amounts at different profits and has a calculator.{/* LINK: once published, link "expat or travel policy" to /health-insurance/expat-health-insurance */}

Some self-employed artists, writers, journalists and designers can join the <Term de="Künstlersozialkasse" />, which covers part of the health and pension contributions. Eligibility is set by the KSK itself.

The choice between public and private can be hard to reverse. An independent licensed broker can look at your own case before you decide.

## Should you charge VAT?

By default, a business charges VAT (<Term de="Umsatzsteuer" />) on its invoices, pays it to the tax office and files VAT returns. The small business rule (<Term de="Kleinunternehmerregelung" />) lets you leave VAT out while your sales stay under two limits. Since 2025, the limits are net sales of €25,000 in the previous year and no more than €100,000 in the current year. If you pass €100,000 during the year, the rule ends with the sale that crosses the line.

<ComparisonTable>

|  | Small business rule | Regular VAT |
|---|---|---|
| VAT on your invoices | None | Yes |
| Reclaim VAT on business purchases | No | Yes |
| Paperwork | Lighter | Heavier |
| Honest downside | You cannot reclaim VAT on equipment and software, and sales limits apply | More paperwork and more deadlines |
| Best for | Low business costs and mostly private customers | High business costs, or business clients who reclaim VAT anyway |

</ComparisonTable>

You make this choice in the questionnaire. If you choose regular VAT even though you qualify for the small business rule, that choice binds you for at least five calendar years.

The article on the [small business VAT exemption](/taxes/kleinunternehmer) explains the start-year rules. [VAT returns explained](/taxes/vat-returns) covers regular VAT.

A VAT ID (<Term de="USt-IdNr" />) is a separate thing. You need one mainly to invoice business clients in other EU countries. The BZSt issues it free of charge, and you can ask for it in the questionnaire or apply later.

## How do you get paid and keep records?

Your **invoices** must carry certain details. A standard invoice must show your tax number or VAT ID, so you need one of them before you send your first. The [invoice article](/taxes/invoice) lists the other required details and has a template.

German law does not require a freelancer who works alone to have a separate **business bank account**. Many open one anyway, because it keeps business and private money apart, and many banks' terms do not allow regular business use of a private account. You can compare options in [business bank accounts for freelancers](/banking/business-bank-accounts).{/* LINK: once published, add: "and read why in [separating business and personal money](/banking/business-vs-personal)." */}

Keep **bookkeeping** from the first day. Record every payment and every expense, and keep the receipts. Most freelancers calculate profit as income minus expenses, called the <Term de="Einnahmenüberschussrechnung" /> (EÜR). [Accounting software](/taxes/accounting-software) can do much of this. The article on [deductible expenses](/taxes/deductible-expenses-for-freelancers) explains what counts.

## What happens with taxes after you start?

You pay income tax on your profit, and you normally file an annual tax return through ELSTER. The tax office can ask you to pay income tax in advance, usually four times a year; [tax prepayments](/taxes/tax-prepayments) explains why and how to lower them. If you charge VAT, you also file VAT returns. If you run a trade, trade tax may apply.

Rates and thresholds change every January; [freelancer numbers](/freelancer-numbers) lists the current ones. A tax advisor (<Term de="Steuerberater" />) can look at your own situation, and [whether you need a tax advisor](/taxes/tax-advisor) explains the costs and how to find an English-speaking one. [Taxes for freelancers in Germany](/taxes/freelancer-taxes) covers the whole system, with this year's deadlines.

## What about pension and other insurance?

Self-employed people in Germany are not automatically part of the public pension system. Some groups must be, for example some teachers and educators, and people who work mainly for one client and have no employees.{/* LINK: once published, add: "The article on [mandatory pension insurance](/insurance/mandatory-pension) lists them, and [pension options](/insurance/pension) covers the choices for everyone else." */}

If you work mainly for one client, follow their instructions and work inside their team, the pension fund can decide you are an employee in disguise (<Term de="Scheinselbstständigkeit" />). That can lead to back payments of social insurance contributions. The German pension fund (<Term de="Deutsche Rentenversicherung" />) runs a procedure that gives a binding answer on your status.

Other insurance, such as liability and disability cover, is mostly a choice. Some professions must carry professional liability insurance.{/* LINK: once published, add: "The article on [which insurance a freelancer needs](/insurance/which-insurance) gives the overview, with more on [liability](/insurance/liability) and [disability insurance](/insurance/disability)." */}
