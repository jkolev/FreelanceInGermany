<ShortAnswer>
    As a freelancer in Germany, you can deduct costs caused by your work, known as <Term de="Betriebsausgaben" />. They lower your profit, so you pay less income tax to the <Term de="Finanzamt" />. You report them once a year in the <Term de="Anlage EÜR" />. What you save is your tax rate applied to the expense, not the full amount on the receipt.
</ShortAnswer>

## What counts as a deductible business expense?

An expense is deductible if your work caused it. It does not have to be indispensable, but it should be reasonable for what you do, and you must be able to show the business reason. Personal living costs stay private, even if they make your working life easier.

If something serves both purposes, you deduct the business share only, such as the percentage of a room or a phone bill that is used for work. Write down how you calculated that share.

Because you track income minus expenses, you generally count an expense in the year you pay it, not the year the invoice arrives. Larger purchases are the exception, as explained below.

<Callout kind="note" title="Keep your invoices clean">
    Ask for invoices made out to you or your business with your correct name and address. Invoices above 250 euros gross must show them, otherwise you may lose the VAT refund. A bank statement alone is rarely enough proof. Since 2025 you may also receive e-invoices, so keep the original file and not just a printout.
</Callout>

## Net or gross: how VAT changes the numbers

If you charge VAT and can reclaim <Term de="Vorsteuer" />, you deduct the net price of each purchase and get the VAT back through your VAT return. If you are a <Term de="Kleinunternehmer" />, you do not charge VAT and cannot reclaim it, so the gross price is your expense. This also decides which price counts against the 800 euro limit for equipment below.

The [small business VAT exemption](/taxes/kleinunternehmer) explains who qualifies and what it means for your invoices.

## How to write off equipment, software, and tech

Anything you buy for work up to 800 euros net can be deducted in full in the year of purchase. These items are called low-value assets (*geringwertige Wirtschaftsgüter*).

More expensive items must normally be spread over their useful life, which is called <Term de="Abschreibung" />. Two options can speed this up. Items between 250 and 1,000 euros net can go into a pool that you write off over five years. For movable assets bought after 30 June 2025 and before 2028, you can also use declining-balance depreciation of up to 30 percent per year.

Computers and software get special treatment. A <Term de="BMF" /> letter from 22 February 2022 allows a useful life of one year for computer hardware, including peripherals such as monitors and printers, and for business software. In practice, you can deduct the full price in the year of purchase, even if you bought the item mid-year and it costs more than 800 euros. This is optional, and it does not cover everything. Smartphones are not on the list, so a phone above 800 euros net is depreciated normally.

Subscriptions for professional software, cloud storage, and project management tools are running costs and fully deductible when you use them for work.

## Claiming home office costs

There are two separate systems, and you cannot combine them for the same period.

* **Daily flat rate:** The <Term de="Homeoffice-Pauschale" /> gives you 6 euros for every day you work mainly from home, up to 210 days and a maximum of 1,260 euros per year. You do not need a separate room, and you do not need receipts.
* **Home study:** You can deduct room costs only if the room is separate, used almost exclusively for work, and the center of your entire professional activity. If you mostly work at client sites, you usually do not qualify. If you do, you choose each year between a proportional share of your actual costs (rent, heating, electricity) and a flat 1,260 euros. Actual costs must be recorded separately, so they only pay off when they exceed the flat amount.

Both amounts apply per person, so two self-employed partners working from the same home each claim their own.

## Business trips, car costs, and commuting

Train tickets, flights, and hotels are deductible at their actual cost when the trip is business-related.

**Your own car for client trips.** Instead of actual costs, you can deduct 0.30 euros for every kilometer you drive for business, however far. If you use the car more than 50 percent for business, it counts as business property. You can then deduct all its costs, but you must account for private use, either with the 1 percent rule or with a <Term de="Fahrtenbuch" />.

**Commuting to an outside workplace.** If you rent an office or a coworking space, the <Term de="Entfernungspauschale" /> applies. Since 1 January 2026 it is 0.38 euros per kilometer of one-way distance, from the first kilometer, for any means of transport. Before 2026, the first 20 kilometers only earned 0.30 euros. If you work from home, there is no commute to deduct.

**Meal allowance on trips.** For trips within Germany, the <Term de="Verpflegungsmehraufwand" /> is 14 euros if you are away more than 8 hours, and for the arrival and departure days of overnight trips. Each full 24-hour day earns 28 euros. These are flat amounts, so the actual restaurant bill does not matter. Trips abroad have country-specific rates.

**Client meals.** You can deduct 70 percent of the reasonable cost, including the tip. You need a <Term de="Bewirtungsbeleg" />: the restaurant invoice plus the date, place, names of all participants, and the business reason, signed by you. Above 250 euros gross, the invoice must name you as the host. Input VAT can usually be reclaimed in full. Your own meals while working are private, and the meal allowance above only covers trips.

**Gifts to clients.** Gifts are deductible up to 50 euros net per recipient and year. This is a hard limit, not an allowance: one cent over, and nothing is deductible. Record who received what.

## Costs freelancers often forget

* Further training, courses, specialist books, and journals
* Business insurance such as professional liability
* Tax adviser fees and accounting software
* Business bank account fees
* Coworking space or office rent
* Website, domain, and marketing

Some professions, such as journalists and freelance teachers, can claim a percentage of their income as flat-rate business expenses instead of listing receipts. Ask a tax adviser whether this applies to you.

## What you cannot deduct

Private costs stay private. This includes groceries, your own rent, fines and penalty charges, and everyday clothing, even if you wear it to work. Only uniforms and protective clothing qualify. For client meals, the 30 percent above the deductible share is lost.

Health insurance, long-term care insurance, and most pension contributions are not business expenses either. You enter them in your tax return as <Term de="Sonderausgaben" />, where they reduce your tax separately from your <Term de="Anlage EÜR" />. Which system to choose is covered in [public vs private health insurance](/health-insurance/public-vs-private).

## How much does a deduction really save?

A deduction lowers your taxable profit, so you save your personal tax rate times the expense, not the whole amount. If you are in public health insurance, a lower profit also means a lower contribution, because it is [calculated from your profit](/calculators/health-insurance-cost).

Say you buy a laptop for 1,190 euros including 19 percent VAT. If you can reclaim VAT, you get 190 euros back through your VAT return and deduct the net 1,000 euros as a business expense. At a personal tax rate of 30 percent, that cuts your income tax by about 300 euros. In total, about 490 euros come back, so the laptop effectively costs you about 700 euros. Your own rate depends on your income.

## How long to keep your receipts

Since 2025, receipts and invoices must be kept for 8 years instead of 10. The period starts at the end of the calendar year in which the document was created. Books and annual accounts still need 10 years, and other business letters 6 years. Many advisers still suggest 10 years for everything tax-related to be safe. E-invoices must be kept as the original digital file.

## How and when to report your expenses

You declare your business expenses once a year with your income tax return, submitted digitally through <Term de="ELSTER" />. Freelancers report their income on <Term de="Anlage S" /> alongside the <Term de="Anlage EÜR" />, which sets your total business income against your total deductible expenses to calculate your profit.

Without a tax adviser, the deadline is 31 July of the following year, which was 31 July 2026 for tax year 2025. With a tax adviser, it extends to the end of February in the second year after the tax year, which was 1 March 2027 for tax year 2025. If you reclaim input VAT, your expenses also show up in your separate VAT returns.
