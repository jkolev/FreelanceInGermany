<ShortAnswer>
As a freelancer in Germany, you pay income tax on your yearly profit and charge VAT on your invoices, unless you qualify as a small business. A freelancer in a liberal profession (<Term de="Freiberufler" />) pays no trade tax.

You register with the tax office (<Term de="Finanzamt" />), usually prepay income tax four times a year, and file returns through <Term de="ELSTER" />. The 2026 return is due on 2 August 2027, or on 29 February 2028 if a tax advisor files it.

From 2027, income tax notices arrive in ELSTER by default, and the one-month deadline to object starts even if you have not opened them.
</ShortAnswer>

## What taxes do freelancers in Germany pay?

Three taxes matter for most freelancers:

- **Income tax** (<Term de="Einkommensteuer" />) is charged on your profit for the calendar year, above a tax-free amount.
- **VAT** (<Term de="Umsatzsteuer" />) is added to most invoices. You collect it from clients and pass it on to the tax office.
- **Trade tax** (<Term de="Gewerbesteuer" />) applies to a trade business (<Term de="Gewerbe" />), but not to a Freiberufler.

Whether you count as a Freiberufler depends on your profession and how you work. Our article on [Freiberufler vs. Gewerbe](/start/freiberufler-vs-gewerbe) explains the difference. If the tax office classes you as Gewerbe, trade tax applies once your trade profit passes 24,500 euros a year.

Two smaller charges are calculated from your income tax: the solidarity surcharge and church tax. A Freiberufler also never needs double-entry bookkeeping, whatever the turnover.

Health, long-term care and pension contributions are not taxes, but they can reduce your taxable income. See our [health insurance guide](/health-insurance/freelancers).

## Which tax numbers do you need?

Three numbers come up often, and they are easy to mix up.

- Your tax ID (<Term de="Steuer-ID" />) has 11 digits. The Federal Central Tax Office (<Term de="BZSt" />) issues one to every resident, and it never changes.
- Your tax number (<Term de="Steuernummer" />) comes from the Finanzamt when you register as a freelancer. You use it for your returns and can show it on invoices.
- Your VAT ID (<Term de="USt-IdNr" />) starts with DE. You request it from the BZSt. You need it for invoices to business clients in other EU countries.

Every standard invoice must show either your tax number or your VAT ID.

## How do you register with the tax office?

When you start freelance work, you must tell the Finanzamt within one month. You do this with the tax registration questionnaire (<Term de="Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung" />), submitted online through ELSTER.

The questionnaire asks about your activity, your start date and your expected profit for the first two years. It also asks whether you want to use the small business scheme and how you want to account for VAT. The Finanzamt uses your profit estimate to set your first tax prepayments.

ELSTER is the tax authorities' free online portal. To register, you need your tax ID, an email address and a way to confirm your identity. If you confirm by post, the activation code arrives by letter, usually within about two weeks; with a German ID card that has the online function switched on, you can confirm online instead. Since 2024 you can switch ELSTER's menus to English, but the forms and the tax office's letters are in German.

The whole process, step by step, is in [how to become a freelancer in Germany](/start/become-a-freelancer).

## What counts as your taxable profit?

Income tax is charged on your profit, not your revenue. Profit is what is left after your business expenses (<Term de="Betriebsausgaben" />).

Freelancers normally calculate profit with a simple income and expense statement (<Term de="Einnahmenüberschussrechnung" />, or EÜR). Money counts in the year it reaches or leaves your account, with a few exceptions. You submit the EÜR electronically with your tax return.

After profit, further deductions apply. Contributions to health, long-term care and some pension schemes count as special expenses (<Term de="Sonderausgaben" />), within limits. They reduce your taxable income, not your profit. The tax rate applies to what remains.

## How much income tax do you pay?

Income tax rises with your taxable income. In 2026, for a single person, it works like this:

- Up to 12,348 euros: no income tax. This is the basic tax-free allowance (<Term de="Grundfreibetrag" />).
- From 12,349 to 17,799 euros: the rate on each extra euro rises from 14% to 24%.
- From 17,800 to 69,878 euros: the rate on each extra euro rises from 24% to 42%.
- From 69,879 euros: 42%.
- From 277,826 euros: 45%.

Each rate applies only to the euros in that range, not to your whole income, so your overall share stays well below your top rate. Married couples who file together pay twice the tax on half their joint income, which doubles the allowance to 24,696 euros.

For 2027, a draft law would raise the allowance to 12,564 euros and change the bands. The cabinet approved it on 2 September 2026; as of mid-November 2026, the Bundestag and Bundesrat had not yet passed it.

To see what your profit means in tax, use our [tax prepayment estimator](/taxes/tax-prepayments), which applies these rates and deducts public health insurance for you. The finance ministry also has a free [income tax calculator](https://www.bmf-steuerrechner.de) (in German). The yearly figures, including the planned 2027 changes, are collected on our [freelancer numbers page](/freelancer-numbers).

Two surcharges are calculated from your income tax. The solidarity surcharge (<Term de="Solidaritätszuschlag" />, or Soli) is 5.5% of your income tax. It applies only if your income tax passes 20,350 euros in 2026 for a single person (40,700 euros for a couple filing jointly), and it phases in gradually above that. Many freelancers do not reach that level.

Church tax (<Term de="Kirchensteuer" />) applies only if you belong to a church that collects it through the state. It is 8% of your income tax in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg and 9% in the other states. The registration questionnaire asks about your religion for this reason.

## Do you pay German tax on all your income?

If you live in Germany, you are normally taxed here on your worldwide income. That includes payments from clients abroad. Living in Germany generally means having a home here or staying for more than six months.

Germany has tax treaties with many countries. They decide which country may tax which income, so the outcome depends on the countries and the type of income involved. The rules also differ in the year you arrive or leave.

## How does VAT work for freelancers?

You add VAT to your invoices. The standard rate is 19%, and most freelance services fall under it. A reduced rate of 7% applies in a few cases, mainly when you grant usage rights under copyright.

You do not keep this money. You pass it on to the Finanzamt, after subtracting the VAT you paid on business purchases (<Term de="Vorsteuer" />). If you paid more VAT than you collected, you get the difference back.

By default, VAT is due for the period in which you do the work, even if the client pays later. A Freiberufler can ask the Finanzamt to pay VAT only when the money arrives (<Term de="Istversteuerung" />), whatever their turnover. The registration questionnaire asks which method you want.

## Can you skip VAT as a small business?

The small business scheme (<Term de="Kleinunternehmerregelung" />) lets you skip VAT if your revenue was no more than 25,000 euros in the previous year and stays at or below 100,000 euros in the current year. These limits apply since 2025. The first year of a business has its own rule.

Under the scheme, you do not charge VAT. You also cannot reclaim the VAT on your purchases, and your invoices need a note that explains why they show no VAT. The full rules are in our article on the [small business VAT exemption](/taxes/kleinunternehmer).

## How often do you file VAT returns?

If you charge VAT, you file advance VAT returns (<Term de="Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung" />) through ELSTER. They are due on the 10th day after each period ends. How often you file depends on the VAT you owed in the previous year:

- More than 9,000 euros: monthly.
- Up to 9,000 euros: quarterly.
- Up to 2,000 euros: the tax office can release you from advance returns.

New businesses used to file monthly in their first two years. That rule is suspended for 2021 to 2026 only, so unless the suspension is extended, it applies again from 2027. You also file an annual VAT return if you charge VAT. See [VAT returns explained](/taxes/vat-returns).

## What about clients abroad?

For business clients in other EU countries, you usually invoice without German VAT. The client accounts for the VAT in their own country under the reverse charge procedure (<Term de="Reverse-Charge-Verfahren" />). You need the client's VAT ID.

You also report these services to the BZSt in an EU sales list (<Term de="Zusammenfassende Meldung" />). Services to clients outside the EU, and to private individuals, follow different rules. Our article on [VAT returns](/taxes/vat-returns) covers the main cases.

## Do you have to issue e-invoices?

Since 1 January 2025, every business in Germany must be able to receive electronic invoices in a structured format, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD. A plain PDF does not count. Small businesses using the Kleinunternehmerregelung must also be able to receive them.

The duty to issue e-invoices to other German businesses starts on 1 January 2027 if your revenue in the previous year was above 800,000 euros. It starts on 1 January 2028 for all other businesses. Small businesses under the Kleinunternehmerregelung are exempt, as are invoices up to 250 euros. See [how to write a German invoice](/taxes/invoice).

## What are income tax prepayments?

The Finanzamt does not wait until the end of the year. Once it has an estimate of your profit, it sets income tax prepayments (<Term de="Steuervorauszahlung" />). At first the estimate comes from your registration questionnaire. Later it comes from your last tax notice.

Prepayments are due on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December. The Finanzamt credits them against your final bill. If they turn out too low, the difference is due after your assessment. If your profit changes a lot, you can ask the Finanzamt to adjust them. See [tax prepayments](/taxes/tax-prepayments).

## Which expenses reduce your tax?

Business expenses reduce your profit, so they reduce your income tax. If you charge VAT, you can also reclaim the VAT on them. Common examples include software, equipment, training, business insurance and a home office.

Each category has limits and conditions. They are set out in [deductible expenses for freelancers](/taxes/deductible-expenses-for-freelancers).

Keep invoices and receipts for every expense, because the Finanzamt can ask for proof. Since 2025, receipts and invoices must be kept for eight years; books and annual accounts for ten. The period starts at the end of the calendar year the document is from.

## What do you file, and when?

Each year, you send the Finanzamt several documents through ELSTER:

- The income tax return (main form ESt 1 A).
- Anlage S, for income from freelance work.
- Anlage EÜR, for your profit calculation.
- Anlage Vorsorgeaufwand, for health, care and pension contributions.
- The annual VAT return, if you charge VAT.

A Gewerbe files Anlage G instead of Anlage S, and a trade tax return once its trade profit is above 24,500 euros. These are the main dates for the 2026 tax year:

- **10 March, 10 June, 10 September, 10 December:** income tax prepayments, if the Finanzamt has set them.
- **10th day after each month or quarter:** advance VAT returns, if you file them.
- **2 August 2027:** annual returns for 2026 if you file them yourself. The usual date, 31 July, is a Saturday.
- **29 February 2028:** annual returns for 2026 if a tax advisor files them.

For the 2025 tax year, the self-filing deadline was 31 July 2026. If a tax advisor files your 2025 return, it is due on 1 March 2027, because 28 February 2027 is a Sunday.

<Callout title="Late returns cost money">
For annual returns, the late-filing surcharge (<Term de="Verspätungszuschlag" />) is 0.25% of the tax you owe after prepayments, for each month you are late, with a minimum of 25 euros a month. The tax office can charge it from the first day you are late, and must charge it once you are 14 months late (19 months with a tax advisor). You can ask the Finanzamt for more time before the deadline.
</Callout>

## What happens after you file?

The Finanzamt sends a tax notice (<Term de="Steuerbescheid" />). It shows your final income tax, subtracts your prepayments and states the amount due. It also sets your prepayments for the next year. Income tax is normally due one month after the notice is delivered, and the due date is printed on the notice.

If you disagree with the notice, you can file an objection (<Term de="Einspruch" />). The deadline is one month after the notice counts as delivered. You can file in writing or through ELSTER.

<Callout title="From 2027, tax notices go to ELSTER by default">
If you have an active ELSTER account, the Finanzamt delivers your income tax notice there from 1 January 2027, not by post. It counts as delivered on the fourth day after it is made available, even if you have not opened it, and your one-month objection period starts on that day. ELSTER sends an email when a notice is ready, so keep your email address current. VAT notices still come by post for now. If you want paper notices, request postal delivery in ELSTER; refusing electronic delivery in 2026 does not count as that request.
</Callout>

## Do you need a tax advisor?

The law does not require freelancers to use one. You can file through ELSTER or tax software yourself. Only licensed tax advisors (<Term de="Steuerberater" />) can give personal tax advice in Germany. Tax associations for employees (Lohnsteuerhilfevereine) cannot help anyone with self-employed income.

A tax advisor who files your return also gives you a later deadline, as shown above. Freelancers often use one in situations like these: setting up in the first year, income from several countries, a mix of employment and freelance income, or a trade business with trade tax. These cases depend on details that a general guide cannot cover. For costs and how to find an English-speaking advisor, see [do you need a tax advisor?](/taxes/tax-advisor)
