<ShortAnswer>
    Health insurance is compulsory in Germany. Freelancers choose between public insurance (<Term de="gesetzliche Krankenversicherung" />) and private insurance (<Term de="private Krankenversicherung" />).

    Public insurance costs a percentage of your profit: in 2026, roughly €270 to €1,260 a month including care insurance, all paid by you. Private insurance is priced by your age and health when you join, and going back to public insurance later is often not possible.

    If you are new to Germany, public insurance may not be open to you. It usually depends on earlier public insurance and on joining within a deadline.
</ShortAnswer>

## Do you have to be insured as a freelancer?

Yes. Everyone living in Germany needs health insurance, and that includes freelancers. Unlike employees, you are not signed up automatically. You choose a scheme and apply yourself.

If you need a residence permit for freelance work, the <Term de="Ausländerbehörde" /> asks for proof of health insurance. Which kind of policy it accepts can vary. [Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/working-in-germany/starting-business/visa/visa-for-freelance-business) advises asking your embassy or foreigners authority before you buy.

This guide is for people whose freelance work is their main job (<Term de="hauptberuflich" /> selbstständig). If you are an employee who freelances on the side, the rules for employees normally apply to you. Insurers look at things such as whether you employ staff and whether your freelance profit clearly exceeds your salary.

## Which kind of insurance can you join?

Freelancers can choose either public or private insurance, whatever they earn. The income limit that applies to employees (the <Term de="Versicherungspflichtgrenze" />, €77,400 a year in 2026) does not apply to you.

You cannot always get into public insurance, though. It depends on your history:

- You are in German public insurance right now, for example as an employee or through a family member (<Term de="Familienversicherung" />). When that cover ends, your <Term de="Krankenkasse" /> automatically continues it as a voluntary membership from the next day, with no minimum insurance period. You can leave only within two weeks of the insurer telling you about this option, and only if you prove you have other cover. This automatic continuation does not apply if the insurer cannot find a residence for you in Germany.
- You were in German public insurance earlier, but that cover has ended. You can join again as a voluntary member if you were insured for 24 months in the last five years, or for 12 months without a break just before it ended, and you apply within three months.
- You were in the public system of another EU or EEA country, or Switzerland. Periods in another country's statutory health insurance generally count like German ones towards the same requirement. Private cover there does not count. Bring proof, ideally the form E104 or its electronic successor S041, which the German insurer can also request from your former insurer. Employment contracts, payslips or tax assessments can also work, and in countries with a national health service, proof of residence. Ask the German insurer to confirm in writing that you can join.
- You come from elsewhere, or you were never in a public scheme. Public insurers can accept you only in limited cases, such as when you can prove statutory insurance abroad. If none of these routes applies, private insurance is usually what remains.
- You work as an artist or publicist, for example as an author, journalist, editor, designer or composer. The <Term de="Künstlersozialkasse" /> (KSK) can make you a compulsory member of public insurance and pays about half of the contribution. Conditions apply, so check the [KSK website](https://www.kuenstlersozialkasse.de/).

    If you have to join public insurance actively, for example after arriving from another EU country, you must tell the insurer within three months of your previous cover ending. Joining later is not possible. If your previous cover was German public insurance, your insurer may have continued it automatically, so check your letters.

## What does public health insurance cost a freelancer?

As a voluntary member you pay the whole contribution yourself. There is no employer share. Three parts add up (2026 rates):

- Health insurance rate: 14.6% if you want <Term de="Krankengeld" /> (public sick pay, explained below), or 14.0% if you do not. You choose when you join.
- <Term de="Zusatzbeitrag" />: an extra rate that each insurer sets itself. The official national average for 2026 is 2.9%. Your insurer's own rate can be higher or lower, and it can change during the year.
- Care insurance (<Term de="Pflegeversicherung" />): 3.6%, or 4.2% if you are 23 or older and have no children. Parents pay 0.25 points less for each child from the second to the fifth, as long as the child is under 25. You have to prove your children to the insurer yourself. Until you do, you pay the childless rate.

Your insurer applies these rates to your monthly income, but never to less than €1,318.33 in 2026. This floor, the <Term de="Mindestbemessungsgrundlage" />, applies even if you earn less or nothing. At the top, contributions stop at €5,812.50 a month (€69,750 a year). That ceiling is the <Term de="Beitragsbemessungsgrenze" />.

Here is what that means in euros per month, using the 2026 average Zusatzbeitrag:

| Rate and situation | Lowest (income up to €1,318.33) | Highest (income from €5,812.50) |
|---|---|---|
| 14.0% rate, with children | €270 | €1,192 |
| 14.0% rate, no children, 23 or older | €278 | €1,226 |
| 14.6% rate, with children | €278 | €1,226 |
| 14.6% rate, no children, 23 or older | €286 | €1,261 |

Figures are calculated from the rates above and rounded to the nearest euro. Your insurer's own Zusatzbeitrag will change them. For amounts at other profits, and how the minimum and maximum work, see [what public health insurance costs freelancers](/health-insurance/public-cost).

German documents write numbers the other way around: 1.318,33 € means €1,318.33.

Contributions for basic health and care cover are deductible from your taxable income as <Term de="Sonderausgaben" />, and in full: the usual €2,800 yearly limit for insurance contributions does not cut them. If you pay the 14.6% rate, which includes a claim to Krankengeld, the deductible part of your health insurance contribution is reduced by 4%. In private insurance, only the share for basic benefits counts, not the share for Krankentagegeld or extras. Your insurer reports the amounts to the tax office, so you don't have to work them out yourself.

## How does the insurer decide what you owe?

Your contribution follows your profit, not your revenue, so every [business expense you deduct](/taxes/deductible-expenses-for-freelancers) lowers it too. The insurer first sets it provisionally, based on your latest <Term de="Einkommensteuerbescheid" />. That is the income tax assessment from the <Term de="Finanzamt" />.

When the assessment for a later year arrives, the insurer recalculates. The result can be a back payment or a refund. The consumer advice centers suggest keeping reserves if your profit is rising.

A few rules matter in practice:

- Other income counts too. Rental income, interest and similar income are added, up to the ceiling.
- New freelancers have no assessment yet. You give the insurer an estimate of your profit or a business analysis. The final figure follows your first assessment.
- If your profit this year is more than 25% below the profit in your last assessment, you can ask for a lower contribution before a new assessment arrives. You prove it with a reduced income tax prepayment notice. The lower amount applies from the month after you apply.
- Contributions are due on the 15th of the following month. Late payment brings surcharges.

Hand in each assessment promptly. If you do not send it after the insurer has reminded you, the insurer can assume the highest possible income and charge the maximum contribution. You have three years after the year in question. For 2023, the deadline is 31 December 2026. This only applies if the insurer has asked you for proof.

## What happens if you cannot work because of illness?

Employees get sick pay from their employer. Freelancers do not, so a long illness can stop your income.

In public insurance, Krankengeld is a replacement payment that starts on the 43rd day of illness, the start of week 7. You have a claim only if you chose the 14.6% rate. For the first 42 days, nothing replaces your income. Krankengeld is 70% of the income your contributions were last based on. Because contributions stop at the ceiling, the most you can get in 2026 is about €135.63 a day (70% of €5,812.50, divided by 30). If you pay on the minimum income base, it is about €31 a day.

Choosing the 14.6% rate binds you to it for three years. You can still change insurer after 12 months, but the new insurer keeps you on the 14.6% rate until the three years are up.

With the 14.0% rate you pay less and have no claim. Some insurers offer an optional tariff (<Term de="Wahltarif" />) on top that pays earlier or at a different level. Such a tariff ties you to the tariff and to the insurer for three years, and during that time you cannot use the special right to leave after a Zusatzbeitrag rise.

In private insurance, income cover is a separate policy called <Term de="Krankentagegeld" />. You choose when it starts and how much it pays.

## How does private health insurance work?

Private insurers set the price from your age and health when you join, plus the benefits and deductible you choose. Your income does not matter. The premium is predictable, and it does not change when your profit does.

Before you join, the insurer asks health questions (<Term de="Gesundheitsprüfung" />). It can decline you, charge a surcharge or exclude certain conditions. Incomplete or wrong answers can later let the insurer cancel the contract, so answers need to be complete and accurate.

The downsides show up over time:

- Premiums tend to rise over your life. A rule of thumb from Stiftung Warentest in 2019 says that a premium for someone joining at 35 roughly triples by retirement age.
- They do not fall when your income falls or when you retire.
- Partners and children need their own contracts. In public insurance, children with little income of their own are covered at no extra cost. A spouse or registered partner with little income is covered at no extra cost until the end of 2027. From 2028, a surcharge of 2.5 percentage points applies for them, unless, for example, you have a child under 12 at home.

Benefits differ too. Private cover often means faster specialist appointments and better dental and hospital options. Public insurance includes things many private tariffs leave out, such as inpatient rehabilitation, more medical aids and psychotherapy.

The <Term de="Basistarif" /> is a private tariff with standard benefits that works as a safety net. Insurers must accept people who are entitled to it, without health questions, surcharges or exclusions. Entitled are, among others, uninsured people who belong to the private system, voluntary public members within six months of becoming free to go private, and private customers whose contract started in 2009 or later. The premium may not exceed the public maximum including the average Zusatzbeitrag: about €1,018 a month in 2026, plus care insurance. Outside the Basistarif, insurers choose their customers.

The consumer advice centers say private cover can make financial sense for single people, and for childless couples who both work. They also say it is not the right choice for everyone.

<Callout title="Private insurance is hard to leave">
    As a self-employed person you have no right to return to public insurance. A return generally needs a job that makes you compulsorily insured, an age under 55, and a salary below the limit (€77,400 in 2026). Registering in another EU country as a way back is, according to the consumer advice centers, largely closed since 1 January 2026.
</Callout>

## Public or private: who does each tend to suit?

<ComparisonTable>

    |  | Public (voluntary) | Private |
    |---|---|---|
    | How the price is set | Share of your profit, between a floor and a ceiling | Your age, health at sign-up, benefits and deductible |
    | If your income falls | Contribution falls, down to the floor | Premium stays the same |
    | If your income rises | Contribution rises, up to the ceiling | Premium stays the same |
    | Partner and children | Free cover if their own income is low (for a spouse, a surcharge from 2028) | Each person needs a contract |
    | Sick pay | Krankengeld from day 43 at the 14.6% rate | Separate Krankentagegeld policy |
    | Getting in | Depends on your insurance history | Insurer can decline, add surcharges or exclude conditions |
    | Going back later | Possible to stay or change insurer | Usually not possible |
    | Main downside | At least about €270 a month even on low income; back payments possible | Premiums tend to rise with age and do not fall in retirement |
    | Best for | Variable or lower income, families with a non-earning partner or children, people who want to keep options open | People in good health with stable income who can carry rising premiums and want private benefits |

</ComparisonTable>

This choice is hard to undo and depends on your health, family plans and income. If you are still weighing it up, our [comparison of public and private insurance](/health-insurance/public-vs-private) sums up which situations favour each. An independent fee-based adviser (<Term de="Versicherungsberater" />, listed through [bvvb.de](https://www.bvvb.de)) or a licensed broker can compare options for your situation. Some [Verbraucherzentralen](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/beratung) offer paid advice as well. For the tax side, ask a tax advisor.

## How do you sign up for public insurance?

1. Check that you can join, and note any three-month deadline.
2. Compare insurers on their Zusatzbeitrag and on the services you use. Public insurers are free to join and their basic benefits are set by law.
3. Apply to your chosen insurer on its form for voluntary members. Forms for the self-employed ask you to pick the 14.6% or the 14.0% rate.
4. Send proof of income: your latest tax assessment, or an estimate of your profit if you are new.
5. Set up payment so each contribution arrives by the 15th of the following month.
6. If you need proof for the foreigners authority, ask the insurer for a confirmation letter.

## Can you switch later?

Between public insurers: after 12 months you can change insurer, with two months' notice to the end of a month. If your insurer raises its Zusatzbeitrag, you can leave earlier: you must cancel by the end of the month the rise takes effect. The notice period still applies, so after a rise on 1 January you move on 1 April.

Since the end of July 2026, insurers no longer have to write to you before they raise the Zusatzbeitrag. Check your insurer's rate yourself, for example at the start of each year, so you don't miss the deadline.

From public to private: you can cancel public insurance, but the cancellation only takes effect once you prove you have other cover.

Between private insurers: you usually lose part of the savings reserves your insurer has built up for you, and you may face new health questions.

From private to public: see the warning above.

## What changes in 2027?

The figures below are the planned values for 2027 as of 2 November 2026. They can still change before 1 January:

- The income values (reference value, minimum base, ceiling and the employee income limit) come from a draft ordinance that the federal government adopted. It needs the approval of the Bundesrat, which usually decides in late November.
- The average Zusatzbeitrag for 2027 has been announced as unchanged at 2.9%. Your insurer still sets its own rate, often in December.
- The higher care insurance surcharge for people without children is part of a reform that the cabinet approved on 30 September 2026. It only applies if the Bundestag and Bundesrat pass it.
- Sick pay: from 1 January 2027, a declaration choosing Krankengeld brings a three-month wait, unless your insurer receives it within 14 days of you joining public insurance as a voluntary member for the first time or of losing sick pay cover through a change of status. This is already law. If you are a freelancer without sick pay and want it, your insurer should receive your declaration by 31 December 2026. [Sick pay for freelancers](/health-insurance/sick-pay) explains the options.
