<ShortAnswer>
Tax prepayments (<Term de="Vorauszahlung" />) are installments on this year's income tax, due to the tax office (<Term de="Finanzamt" />) on 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December. The office sets the amounts in a notice (<Term de="Vorauszahlungsbescheid" />), usually based on your last tax assessment, and credits what you pay against your final bill. If your income has fallen, you can ask for lower amounts, but the current notice stays in force until you get a new one. A first assessment can also bring a bill for last year and prepayments for this year at the same time.
</ShortAnswer>

## Why did I get a prepayment notice?

Employees have income tax taken from every paycheck. Nobody does that for freelance income, so the tax office collects it in advance instead. It sets prepayments for the current year and expects you to pay them in four parts.

The notice usually arrives together with your income tax assessment (<Term de="Steuerbescheid" />) for the year before. In your first years, that often means two bills at once: the tax for last year and prepayments for this one.

<Callout title="A first assessment can bring two bills at once">
The tax for the past year is due within one month of receiving the assessment. Prepayments for the current year can fall due at the same time, sometimes with higher amounts that make up for dates already passed. Read the due dates on every notice the day it arrives.
</Callout>

The office can also set prepayments before your first assessment, based on the profit you estimated in [the questionnaire you filled in when you registered](/start/tax-registration-questionnaire).

Not everyone receives them. The office sets prepayments only if they come to at least €400 for the year and at least €100 per due date. If your taxable income (your profit minus allowed deductions) stays below the basic allowance (<Term de="Grundfreibetrag" />), you owe no income tax at all. In 2026 that allowance is €12,348; it is adjusted most years.

## How does the tax office set the amount?

The starting point is the income tax from your most recent assessment, minus any tax already withheld, for example from a salary. The office splits the yearly amount into four parts. As a made-up example, an assessed tax of €6,000 would give four prepayments of €1,500.

The notice can also include the solidarity surcharge and church tax, if they apply to you.

The amount reflects an earlier year, not this one. If your income has changed since, the prepayments can be too high or too low. The office can adjust them in either direction.

## When do I pay, and how?

The four due dates are set by law: 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December. The notice lists the amount for each date and the tax office's bank details. Don't count on a reminder, because the notice is the payment request.

You can pay by bank transfer or give the office a direct debit mandate (<Term de="SEPA-Lastschriftmandat" />). Use the tax number (<Term de="Steuernummer" />) and payment reference printed on the notice. A standing order needs updating whenever the amounts change.

## What if the notice arrives late in the year?

Assessments often arrive in the second half of the year, after some due dates have passed. How the office catches up depends on timing:

- **A due date is still ahead this year:** the office can raise the next prepayment to cover the shortfall, with no notice period. That can make a single quarter noticeably larger.
- **No due date is left:** the office adds the increase to the last prepayment of the year, and you pay it within one month of receiving the notice. It does this only if the increase is at least €5,000.

If you can't pay a large catch-up amount at once, you can ask the tax office for a deferral (Stundung).

## Can you get the amount lowered?

Yes, you can ask. Section 37 of the Income Tax Act (EStG) lets the tax office adjust prepayments to the tax you are expected to owe for the year. It can do this until the end of the 15th month after the year ends. The office decides, and it can lower the amounts by less than you asked, or refuse.

Requests usually rest on something that changed since the year the office used as its basis. Examples are lower income, a loss, a large one-off payment from a client in that base year, or big business expenses this year. See [deductible expenses for freelancers](/taxes/deductible-expenses-for-freelancers) for what counts.

To make a request:

1. Check the year and amounts on your current notice.
2. Estimate your profit for this year: income received and expected, minus business expenses.
3. Send the request through the tax office's online portal (<Term de="ELSTER" />). In Mein ELSTER the form is called "Antrag auf Anpassung von Vorauszahlungen", under Formulare & Leistungen, then Alle Formulare. A letter also works.
4. Include your tax number, the year, the amount you propose and your reasons. Attach the forecast and evidence the office can check, such as sales so far or a contract that has ended.
5. Wait for a new notice. Allow at least two weeks before the next due date.

Until the new notice arrives, the current one stays in force and its amounts stay due. Church tax and the solidarity surcharge follow the income tax automatically.

Setting the amount too low has a cost: you pay the difference with your final bill, and interest can apply (see below). If the amounts are large or your income is hard to predict, a [tax advisor](/taxes/tax-advisor) can prepare the forecast and the request.

## What happens if you pay late or pay too little?

<Callout title="Paying late costs 1% a month">
If a prepayment arrives after its due date, the tax office adds a late-payment surcharge (<Term de="Säumniszuschlag" />) of 1% of the unpaid amount for each month started. The amount is rounded down to a multiple of €50. Bank transfers get a three-day grace period, and the day that counts is the day the money reaches the tax office's account. The grace period doesn't apply to cash or cheques.
</Callout>

Paying too little is different. If your prepayments fall short of the final tax, you pay the difference after your assessment. Interest on that difference (<Term de="Nachzahlungszinsen" />) starts 15 months after the end of the tax year. For 2026, that is 1 April 2028.

The rate is 0.15% per full month, or 1.8% a year. A draft of the Annual Tax Act 2026 would double it to 0.3% a month for interest periods from 1 January 2027. As of mid-November 2026, the Bundestag and Bundesrat had not yet passed it.

You can avoid most of that interest by paying early. If you expect a back-payment, you can transfer it voluntarily before your assessment arrives. Once the office has credited the payment against the final tax, no interest is charged from the day it arrived. Ask your tax office how to label such a payment, so it is booked to the right tax and year.

## Can you object to the notice?

Yes. A prepayment notice is a formal decision, and it ends with instructions on how to object (<Term de="Einspruch" />). You have one month from receiving it. A posted notice counts as received on the fourth day after it was sent. An objection does not pause the payments; for that, you would have to apply for a suspension separately.

## How do prepayments fit with your tax return?

As a freelancer you file an annual income tax return. The office compares the tax for the year with what you prepaid. If you prepaid more, you get the difference back. If you prepaid less, you pay the difference.

The office already has your payments on your tax account, so you don't enter them in your return. See [taxes for freelancers](/taxes/freelancer-taxes) for the whole system.

## Are these the same as VAT prepayments?

No. Income tax prepayments are installments on your income tax. VAT advance returns (<Term de="Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung" />) report the VAT you charged clients and have their own deadlines; see [VAT returns explained](/taxes/vat-returns).

If you run a trade business rather than a freelance one, you can also receive separate trade tax (<Term de="Gewerbesteuer" />) prepayments. See [Freiberufler vs. Gewerbe](/start/freiberufler-vs-gewerbe) for the difference.
