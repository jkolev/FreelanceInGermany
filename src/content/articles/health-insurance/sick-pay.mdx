<ShortAnswer>
Freelancers in Germany get no sick pay unless they arrange it. In public health insurance, you have to choose statutory sick pay (<Term de="Krankengeld" />) in writing. It then pays from the 43rd day of illness, and nothing before that. In private insurance, you need a separate daily allowance policy.

From 1 January 2027, choosing public sick pay later brings a three-month wait. If you are already a freelancer without it and want it, make sure your insurer receives your declaration by 31 December 2026.
</ShortAnswer>

## Do freelancers get sick pay in Germany?

Not automatically. Employees receive their normal pay from their employer for up to six weeks of illness. After that, public insurance pays sick pay. Freelancers have no employer, so neither step happens by default.

Your rent, software and insurance contributions keep running while your income stops. What you can claim depends on how you are insured:

- **Public insurance:** sick pay is optional. You have to opt in.
- **Private insurance:** there is no statutory sick pay. You buy a daily allowance policy.

If you are an employee with freelance income on the side, your Krankengeld is based on your salary only. Your freelance income is not included.

## What does public insurance pay a freelancer?

Public insurers pay Krankengeld to a freelancer only if you choose it. The choice is called a <Term de="Wahlerklärung" />. It is a written statement to your <Term de="Krankenkasse" /> that your membership includes sick pay. It applies to people who are self-employed as their main occupation and voluntarily insured in public insurance.

In 2026, the main rules are:

- **Start:** Krankengeld begins on day 43 of illness, the first day of week 7. The insurer pays nothing before that.
- **Amount:** 70% of the daily income your contributions are based on. The maximum is €135.63 per day.
- **Cost:** the general contribution rate of 14.6% applies instead of the reduced rate of 14.0%, plus your insurer's <Term de="Zusatzbeitrag" />.
- **Commitment:** you are bound to the choice for three years.
- **Duration:** up to 78 weeks within three years for the same illness, counted from the first day you cannot work.

For example, if your contributions are based on €3,000 a month, Krankengeld comes to about €70 per day. The extra 0.6 percentage points of contribution cost about €18 a month.

The daily maximum rises with the contribution ceiling. From 1 January 2027, the ceiling is €6,375 a month, which raises the maximum to €148.75 a day. See [freelancer numbers](/freelancer-numbers) for all 2027 figures.

Krankengeld is tax-free. Your insurer reports it to the <Term de="Finanzamt" />, though, and it can raise the tax rate on your other income (<Term de="Progressionsvorbehalt" />).

Members of the <Term de="Künstlersozialkasse" /> (KSK), the social insurance fund for artists and writers, are insured differently. Their insurance includes Krankengeld from the seventh week without a separate declaration. An optional tariff can make it start earlier, at the latest from the third week.

For how contributions are calculated, see [how much public health insurance costs freelancers](/health-insurance/public-cost).

### How do you choose it?

1. Ask your insurer for its Wahlerklärung form, or use its online option if it has one.
2. Send it in a way that proves the insurer received it, such as the insurer's online portal or a letter with proof of delivery.
3. Ask the insurer to confirm in writing the date your cover starts.

## What changes on 1 January 2027?

<Callout title="New waiting period from 1 January 2027">
For a Wahlerklärung your insurer receives from 1 January 2027, Krankengeld cover starts only after a three-month wait. The wait is skipped in two cases, if the declaration arrives within 14 days. Illness during the wait can delay your cover further.
</Callout>

Until the end of 2026, a Wahlerklärung takes effect as soon as your insurer receives it. The exception is illness: a declaration received while you are ill takes effect only on the day after that illness ends.

The GKV-Beitragssatzstabilisierungsgesetz of 24 July 2026 adds a wait of three months after your insurer receives the declaration. The wait does not apply in two cases, if the declaration reaches your insurer within 14 days of the event:

- You become a voluntary member of public insurance for the first time.
- You had Krankengeld cover until now and lost it because your status changed, for example when you leave a job with compulsory insurance to work as a freelancer.

The 14 days run from the start of your voluntary membership or from the change of status. What counts is the day the insurer receives the declaration, not the day you send it.

Other points to know:

- If you fall ill during the three months, your cover starts only on the day after that illness ends, even if the three months are over by then.
- The wait does not apply if an accident during the wait causes the illness, or causes hospital or rehabilitation treatment paid by your insurer.
- The law has no special transition rule. The wait applies to declarations your insurer receives from 1 January 2027. If you already chose Krankengeld, you do not need to choose it again.
- Maternity pay for self-employed women (Mutterschaftsgeld) is paid at the level of Krankengeld and requires a Krankengeld entitlement. If you are planning a family and your declaration is still in its waiting period, ask your insurer how this affects you.

## How can you cover the first six weeks?

Public Krankengeld does not pay before day 43. Three things can fill the gap:

- A savings buffer. It has no premium, but it only lasts as long as the money does.
- An optional sick pay tariff from your public insurer (<Term de="Wahltarif" />). It pays earlier, often from day 15 or 22, or tops up the statutory amount. It has its own premium and terms.
- A private daily allowance policy (<Term de="Krankentagegeld" />). It also works alongside public insurance.

<ComparisonTable>

| | Statutory Krankengeld | Optional tariff (Wahltarif) | Private daily allowance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Works with | Voluntary public insurance | Public insurance | Public or private insurance |
| Pays from | Day 43 | Earlier than day 43; start differs by insurer | The waiting period you choose |
| Amount | 70% of your contribution income, capped (€135.63 per day in 2026) | Set by the tariff; some top up the statutory amount | You choose, up to your net income |
| Cost | 14.6% instead of 14.0%, plus Zusatzbeitrag | Separate premium set by the insurer | Depends on age, health, job, amount and waiting period |
| Main downside | Nothing for 42 days; three-year commitment; wait from 2027 | Terms differ by insurer; three-year minimum commitment | Health questions and exclusions; ends if you are classed as unable to work in your profession |
| Best for | People who want the statutory baseline and can cover the first six weeks | People in public insurance who want payment before day 43 | People in private insurance, or in public insurance who want a higher amount or an earlier start |

</ComparisonTable>

The new three-month wait is written into the rules for the Wahlerklärung. Wahltarife follow each insurer's own terms, and many already have a waiting period of their own, so check the terms before you sign.

## How does private insurance handle sick pay?

Private insurers do not pay Krankengeld at all. If you are privately insured, sick pay comes from a Krankentagegeld policy. It can be part of your health insurance contract or a separate one.

You agree the daily amount when you sign up. It cannot be higher than your net income. You also choose the waiting period before payment starts (<Term de="Karenzzeit" />). A shorter wait means a higher premium.

Insurers ask health questions and can exclude conditions. Under the standard model terms, payment has no fixed time limit while you are unable to work. The policy ends, though, once you are classed as unable to work in your profession (<Term de="Berufsunfähigkeit" />): more than 50% unable to do your previous job, with no end in sight. If you are already receiving payments at that point, they continue for up to three months. Check each policy's own terms, because they can differ from the model terms.

Disability insurance is a different product. It pays a monthly amount for long-term inability to work in your profession, which is where daily allowance stops.

Private daily allowance is tax-free, and unlike Krankengeld it does not raise the tax rate on your other income.

Comparing private policies is a decision where an independent licensed broker can help. For the wider choice, see [public vs. private health insurance for freelancers](/health-insurance/public-vs-private).

## What do you need to do when you are ill?

1. See a doctor and ask for a medical certificate (<Term de="Arbeitsunfähigkeitsbescheinigung" />). Sick pay needs a doctor's confirmation that you cannot work.
2. Keep the certificates continuous. If you are still ill, get the next certificate no later than the next working day after the last certified day. Saturdays do not count as working days, so if a certificate ends on a Friday, Monday is still in time.
3. Ask your insurer how it receives the certificate. Doctors usually send it electronically. In other cases, you submit it yourself.
4. Expect payment after the fact. For example, TK pays Krankengeld in arrears, once a new certificate is issued or you are well again.
