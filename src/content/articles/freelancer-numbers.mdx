<ShortAnswer>
These are the 2027 thresholds and rates that matter most to freelancers in Germany. Public health insurance is planned to calculate your contribution on income up to €6,375 a month, and on at least €1,376.67 even if you earn less; the Bundesrat votes on these figures on 20 November 2026. The 2027 income tax tariff is still a bill, so treat those figures as drafts. The small business VAT limits stay at €25,000 (previous year) and €100,000 (current year), both without VAT.
</ShortAnswer>

## How much does public health insurance cost in 2027?

If you are voluntarily insured in the public system, you pay the whole contribution yourself. Your insurer applies a percentage to your total income, between a minimum and a ceiling. The ceiling is the <Term de="Beitragsbemessungsgrenze" />.

The minimum is a third of the <Term de="Bezugsgröße" />, a reference figure the government resets each year. If your income is lower, the insurer still calculates your contribution on the minimum.

Two base rates exist. The standard 14.6% includes entitlement to <Term de="Krankengeld" />, your insurer's sick pay. The reduced 14.0% applies if you have no such entitlement.

Each insurer also adds its own <Term de="Zusatzbeitrag" /> to the base rate. The 2.9% below is only the official average, so your rate can be higher or lower.

| | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Health insurance ceiling, per month | €5,812.50 | €6,375 |
| Health insurance ceiling, per year | €69,750 | €76,500 |
| Care insurance ceiling, per month | €5,812.50 | €6,075, or €6,375 if the care bill passes |
| Minimum income used for your contribution, per month | €1,318.33 | €1,376.67 |
| Base rate with sick pay entitlement | 14.6% | 14.6% |
| Base rate without sick pay entitlement | 14.0% | 14.0% |
| Average additional rate | 2.9% | 2.9% |
| Long-term care rate | 3.6% | 3.6% |
| Extra care rate if you are 23 or older and have no children | 0.6% | 0.6%, or 0.9% if the care bill passes |
| Income limit for free family insurance, per month | €565 (€603 with a minijob) | €590 (€633 with a minijob) |
| Employee income limit for opting out of public insurance, per year | €77,400 | €84,150 |

For 2027, the health insurance ceiling rises by an extra €300 a month on top of the usual wage-based increase. Under current law, that extra step does not apply to care insurance, so for the first time since 2017 the two ceilings would differ. The care bill would align them again.

The care rate is lower if you have two or more children. The last row is the <Term de="Versicherungspflichtgrenze" />. It applies to employees, so it can matter if you hold a job alongside your freelance work.

### What these rates mean in euros

| Monthly contribution in 2027, at the average 2.9% rate | On €1,376.67 (minimum) | At the ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Health insurance with sick pay (17.5%) | about €241 | about €1,116 |
| Health insurance without sick pay (16.9%) | about €233 | about €1,077 |
| Long-term care insurance (3.6%) | about €50 | about €219, or €230 if the care bill passes |

If you are 23 or older and have no children, add the care surcharge from the first table. Your insurer's own rate decides your real contribution. For a calculation with your own income, see [how much public health insurance costs freelancers](/health-insurance/public-cost).

Private insurance premiums depend on your age, health and tariff, so no single number applies. [Public vs. private health insurance](/health-insurance/public-vs-private) explains how the two systems differ.

## How much do you pay into the public pension in 2027?

Most freelancers are not required to pay into the public pension. Some groups must, such as self-employed teachers and coaches.

People who must pay usually pay a fixed monthly amount, the <Term de="Regelbeitrag" />. They can ask to pay on their actual profit instead. Everyone else can pay voluntarily, between a minimum and the ceiling.

The 2027 amounts below are calculated from the planned 2027 figures, assuming the contribution rate stays at 18.6%.

| | 2026 | 2027 (planned) |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution rate | 18.6% | 18.6% |
| Income ceiling, per month | €8,450 | €8,850 |
| Reference figure, per month | €3,955 | €4,130 |
| Standard contribution if you must pay, per month | €735.63 | €768.18 |
| Half contribution in the first years, per month | €367.82 | €384.09 |
| Voluntary contribution, minimum and maximum per month | €112.16 and €1,571.70 | €117.74 and €1,646.10 |
| Most you can deduct for pension contributions, single | €30,826 | €32,308 |

The half contribution is available until the end of the third calendar year after the year you start. The last row covers public pension contributions and contributions to a <Term de="Basisrente" /> together, and it is one overall limit, not a separate allowance. Couples assessed jointly get double, and 100% of the contributions count.

## What are the income tax rates in 2027?

Income tax applies to your taxable income. For a freelancer, that is your profit (revenue minus business expenses), minus other deductions. Between the allowance and the 42% threshold, the rate on each extra euro rises gradually from 14% to 42%.

<Callout title="The 2027 tariff is not law yet">
The 2027 column below comes from a government bill. Parliament held its first reading on 8 October 2026, and the numbers can change before it passes. The 2026 column is the tariff in force today.
</Callout>

Your first euros of taxable income are tax free. That allowance is the <Term de="Grundfreibetrag" />.

| | 2026 (in force) | 2027 (bill) |
|---|---|---|
| Tax-free allowance, single | €12,348 | €12,564 |
| Tax-free allowance, couples assessed jointly | €24,696 | €25,128 |
| Lowest rate | 14% | 14% |
| 42% rate starts at taxable income of | €69,879 | €70,601 |
| 45% rate starts at | €277,826 | €250,000 |
| New 47% rate starts at | none | €280,000 |

The amounts are for a single person. For couples assessed jointly, double them. To see what your profit means in tax, use our [tax prepayment estimator](/taxes/tax-prepayments), which uses the 2026 tariff.

The <Term de="Solidaritätszuschlag" /> is 5.5% of your income tax. It applies only once your income tax passes €20,350 as a single person (€40,700 for couples assessed jointly), with a phase-in zone above that. The bill leaves these limits unchanged for 2027.

<Term de="Kirchensteuer" /> is 8% of income tax in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg and 9% elsewhere. It applies only if you belong to a church that collects it through the state.

[Taxes for freelancers in Germany](/taxes/freelancer-taxes) explains how these taxes fit together.

## When does VAT apply, and how often do you file?

The <Term de="Kleinunternehmerregelung" /> lets you skip charging VAT if two limits hold. Your revenue last year was at most €25,000, and your revenue this year stays at or below €100,000. Both limits exclude VAT and have applied since 2025.

If you pass €100,000 during the year, regular VAT applies from the sale that crosses the line. [The small business VAT exemption](/taxes/kleinunternehmer) explains the details. The standard VAT rate is 19%. A reduced rate of 7% applies to a limited list of goods and services; for freelancers, mainly when you grant usage rights under copyright.

If you charge VAT, you file an advance VAT return (<Term de="Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung" />) during the year. How often depends on the VAT you owed last year.

| VAT you owed last year | How often you file |
|---|---|
| More than €9,000 | Monthly |
| More than €2,000, up to €9,000 | Quarterly |
| €2,000 or less | The tax office can release you. You then file only the annual return |

Each return is due on the 10th of the month after the period, or the next working day. A <Term de="Dauerfristverlängerung" /> moves each deadline back one month. Monthly filers who use it also make a special prepayment of one eleventh of the previous year's VAT. [VAT returns explained](/taxes/vat-returns) walks through the form.

<Callout title="New businesses: monthly filing returns in 2027">
For tax years 2021 to 2026, new businesses did not have to file monthly in their first two years. The current law ends that exception after 2026, and as of 18 November 2026 no extension has been passed. A business that starts in 2027 would file monthly in 2027 and 2028. If you started in 2026, 2027 is your second year, so you would file monthly in 2027 too. Your tax office tells you which rhythm applies.
</Callout>

## Does trade tax apply to you?

<Term de="Freiberufler" /> do not pay <Term de="Gewerbesteuer" />. If the tax office treats your work as a <Term de="Gewerbe" />, you do. [Freiberufler vs. Gewerbe](/start/freiberufler-vs-gewerbe) explains the difference.

| Trade tax item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Yearly allowance on trade profit (sole proprietors and partnerships of individuals) | €24,500 |
| Base rate on profit above the allowance | 3.5% |
| Municipal multiplier | Set by each municipality, at least 200% |
| Full accounts required for trade businesses above | €800,000 revenue or €80,000 profit a year |

Your trade tax is the profit above the allowance, times 3.5%, times your municipality's multiplier. Part of it is credited against your income tax. Freiberufler can use the simple profit statement whatever their revenue.

## Which invoice rules change in 2027?

Every business in Germany has had to be able to receive e-invoices since 1 January 2025. An <Term de="E-Rechnung" /> is structured data in a set format, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD. A plain PDF does not count.

Issuing e-invoices to other businesses in Germany becomes mandatory in two steps:

- 1 January 2027, if your revenue in 2026 was above €800,000
- 1 January 2028, for everyone else

Some invoices are exempt: those from businesses using the small business VAT exemption, invoices up to €250 including VAT, and invoices to private customers. [How to write a German invoice](/taxes/invoice) covers the required details.

## Which dates matter in 2027?

| What | Due in 2027 |
|---|---|
| Income tax prepayments, if the tax office has set them | 10 March, 10 June, 10 September and 10 December |
| Monthly advance VAT return | The 10th of the next month, or one month later with the extension |
| Quarterly advance VAT return | 11 January (for Q4 2026), 12 April, 12 July and 11 October |
| Annual tax returns for 2026, if you file yourself | 2 August 2027 (31 July is a Saturday) |
| Annual tax returns for 2026, with a tax advisor | 29 February 2028 |

The quarterly dates move because the 10th falls on a weekend in those months.

The tax office sets income tax prepayments only if you are expected to owe at least €400 a year, and at least €100 per payment. [Tax prepayments](/taxes/tax-prepayments) explains why they arrive and how to lower them.

If you file your annual return late, the tax office can add a late filing surcharge of 0.25% of the tax still due for each month you are late, with a minimum of €25 a month. Once you are 14 months late (19 months with a tax advisor), it must charge it.

Back taxes from your assessment earn interest starting 15 months after the end of the tax year. The rate is 0.15% a month, 1.8% a year. The Jahressteuergesetz 2026 bill would double it to 0.3% a month from 1 January 2027, also for older tax years where interest runs after that date.
