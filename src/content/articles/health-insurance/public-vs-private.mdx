<ShortAnswer>
As a freelancer you must have health insurance, and you can often choose. **Public** insurance charges a share of your income and usually covers your children for free. **Private** insurance is priced on your age and health, and can be cheaper when you are young and healthy.

The catch: once you go private, getting back into the public system is difficult, and from 55 it is generally no longer possible.
</ShortAnswer>

## How the two systems differ

Germany has two parallel systems. Public insurers are each called a <Term de="Krankenkasse" />, together the <Term de="GKV" />, and the private system is known as <Term de="PKV" />. Tap a German term to see what it means.

<ComparisonTable>
|                 | Public (GKV)                          | Private (PKV)                          |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Price based on  | Your income                           | Your age, health and chosen plan       |
| Children        | Usually covered at no extra cost      | Each child needs a paid plan           |
| Health check    | None                                  | Yes, can mean surcharges or refusal    |
| Sick pay        | Optional, via the general rate        | Optional, as an add-on                 |
| Switching later | You can leave for private             | Returning to public is restricted      |
| Best for        | Families, health conditions, modest or changing income | Young, healthy, high earners staying self-employed |
</ComparisonTable>

## Can you choose?

Not always. To join public insurance voluntarily as a freelancer, you usually need [enough previous insurance periods](/health-insurance/freelancers#which-kind-of-insurance-can-you-join) in a public system. Periods in other EU countries often count. If you come from outside the EU and start freelancing straight away, private insurance may be your only option at first.

<Callout title="Before you apply for a visa">
The immigration office will ask for proof of health insurance. Arrange it before your appointment, not after.
</Callout>

## What it costs you

Public contributions are a percentage of your profit, between [a minimum and a ceiling](/calculators/health-insurance-cost#key-numbers). As a freelancer you pay the full amount yourself, with no employer share. Private premiums do not depend on income, but tend to rise as you get older. ## Switching back is hard

The public system has [strict rules for returning](/health-insurance/freelancers#can-you-switch-later) once you leave it. For freelancers, the usual way back is becoming an employee below the compulsory insurance income limit, and from age 55 that door is generally closed. Treat private insurance as a long-term decision, not a way to save money for a few years.

## Which one fits you

- **Public** often fits if you plan to have children, have a health condition, expect a modest or changing income, or may not stay in Germany for long.
- **Private** can fit if you are young and healthy, earn well above average, and plan to stay self-employed for many years.

## Where to get help choosing

The choice is hard to undo, so it is worth getting advice on your own case. An independent fee-based adviser (<Term de="Versicherungsberater" />) charges you directly and has no commission at stake. Brokers and agents are paid by insurers, which is worth knowing when you compare.
