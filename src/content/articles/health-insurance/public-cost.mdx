<ShortAnswer>
    In 2026, a freelancer in public health insurance pays roughly 20.5 to 21.7 percent of profit, with no employer share. The exact rate depends on sick pay, on whether you have children, and on your insurer's own extra contribution. At the 2.9% average extra contribution, the bill is about 270 to 286 euros a month at the lowest and 1,190 to 1,260 euros at the highest. Your insurer bills a provisional amount first and settles after your tax assessment, and it can charge the maximum if you don't send proof of income.
</ShortAnswer>

## How is the monthly amount calculated?

Your contribution is a percentage of your profit, up to a ceiling. As a freelancer you are a voluntary member (<Term de="freiwillig versichert" />) of a public insurer (<Term de="Krankenkasse" />), so you pay the whole amount yourself. An employee would split it with an employer.

It has three parts. These are the 2026 rates:

- **Base rate:** 14.6% of profit, or 14.0% if you don't include sick pay (explained below).
- **Extra contribution** (<Term de="Zusatzbeitrag" />): set by each insurer. The official average for 2026 is 2.9%. In October 2026, rates across 93 insurers ranged from 2.18% to 4.39%, and the actual average was about 3.1%.
- **Care insurance** (<Term de="Pflegeversicherung" />): 3.6%, or 4.2% if you are 23 or older and have no children. With one child it stays at 3.6%, and it falls further for each additional child under 25.

## What will you pay at different profits?

The table shows the monthly total for health and care insurance in 2026. It assumes the 2.9% average extra contribution and the 4.2% care rate for people without children. Amounts are rounded.

| Monthly profit | Without sick pay | With sick pay |
|---|---|---|
| 1,318 € or less | 278 € | 286 € |
| 2,000 € | 422 € | 434 € |
| 3,000 € | 633 € | 651 € |
| 4,000 € | 844 € | 868 € |
| 5,000 € | 1,055 € | 1,085 € |
| 5,812.50 € or more | 1,226 € | 1,261 € |

Profit means revenue minus [business expenses](/taxes/deductible-expenses-for-freelancers), as in your tax return. If you have a child, care insurance is 0.6 points lower. That is 6 euros less for every 1,000 euros of profit.

Your insurer's extra contribution moves the total too. At 3,000 euros of profit, one percentage point is 30 euros a month. To try your own numbers, use the calculator.

## What are the minimum and the maximum?

**Minimum.** The law sets a floor for the income your contribution is based on. It is one-third of the monthly reference figure for social insurance (<Term de="Bezugsgröße" />), which changes each January. In 2026 that is 1,318.33 euros. If your profit is lower, or you make a loss, you still pay on that amount. The total is about 270 to 286 euros a month, depending on sick pay and children.

**Maximum.** The income ceiling (<Term de="Beitragsbemessungsgrenze" />) is 5,812.50 euros a month, or 69,750 euros a year, in 2026. Profit above it isn't charged. The total is then about 1,190 to 1,260 euros a month.

## Which income counts, and when is it checked?

Contributions follow your profit as shown in your tax assessment (<Term de="Einkommensteuerbescheid" />). Other income, such as rent from a property you let, can count as well.

The tax assessment arrives long after the income was earned. So the insurer works in steps:

1. When you start freelancing, it sets a provisional amount based on the income you expect and can document.
2. Once you have a tax assessment, it bases the provisional amount on your latest one, from the month after that assessment is issued.
3. When the assessment for a given year arrives, it fixes that year's contribution for good. You pay any difference or get a refund.

A strong year can therefore bring a back payment later, and a weak year a refund.

<Callout title="No proof of income can mean the maximum bill">

    If your insurer asks for proof of your income and you don't send it, it can charge the maximum: about 1,190 to 1,260 euros a month in 2026. The same applies if you don't prove your actual income for a year within three years after that year ends.

    If the tax office (<Term de="Finanzamt" />) hasn't issued your assessment yet, a confirmation from it pauses this for twelve months. If you were charged the maximum and then send proof, you can ask for a recalculation. The request must come within twelve months of the insurer notifying you.

</Callout>

If your profit this year is more than 25% lower than the profit your contribution is based on, you can ask for a lower provisional amount before your next assessment arrives. You prove it with a reduced income tax prepayment notice (<Term de="Vorauszahlung" />) from the tax office. The lower amount applies from the month after you apply and send the notice, and it is settled later like any other provisional amount. A drop in other income, such as rent, does not count for this.

## Do you pay extra for sick pay?

Public health insurance doesn't automatically include sick pay (<Term de="Krankengeld" />) for freelancers. By default you pay the lower rate of 14.0% and have no claim. To include sick pay, you submit a written declaration (<Term de="Wahlerklärung" />) and pay 14.6%. Statutory sick pay then starts on day 43 of illness, in the seventh week.

<ComparisonTable>

    | | No sick pay | Statutory sick pay | Earlier sick pay (insurer plan or private policy) |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Base rate | 14.0% | 14.6% | 14.0% or 14.6%, plus a premium |
    | Sick pay starts | Not covered | Day 43 of illness | Set by the plan or policy |
    | Main downside | Nothing replaces your income if you are ill | You cover the first six weeks yourself, and you are bound to the 14.6% rate for three years | Costs more, and conditions differ by provider |
    | Best for | People with savings or another way to cover lost income | People who want a basic level of sick pay inside the public system | People whose costs continue even during a short illness |

</ComparisonTable>

If you are weighing early sick pay plans or private policies, an independent licensed insurance broker or advisor can compare them for your situation.

<Callout title="From 2027, choosing sick pay late means a wait">

    A change to the sick pay rules takes effect on 1 January 2027. Protection starts without a wait if your declaration reaches the insurer within 14 days of one of these:

    - you become a voluntary member of public insurance for the first time, or
    - you lose an existing sick pay claim because your status changed, for example when you leave a job to go freelance.

    In all other cases there is a three-month wait after the insurer receives your declaration. If you fall ill during that time, protection starts only after the illness ends, unless an accident during the wait caused the illness. What counts is the day the insurer receives your declaration, not the day you post it.

</Callout>

## Does your family cost extra?

Until the end of 2027, no. Insurers aren't allowed to grade contributions for voluntary members by marital status or number of dependents (§ 240 SGB V).

Your spouse and children can be covered through family co-insurance (<Term de="Familienversicherung" />) if they meet the conditions. A spouse, for example, generally needs a very low income, at most one-seventh of the reference figure (565 euros a month in 2026), and can't be self-employed full time. Conditions for children differ.

**From 2028, a co-insured spouse costs extra.** A law passed in July 2026 adds a surcharge of 2.5 percentage points to your rate for a spouse or registered partner in family co-insurance. At 3,000 euros of profit, that is 75 euros a month. There are exceptions, for example if you have a child under 12 living at home, if your spouse cares for a relative, or if your spouse has reached retirement age. Children stay covered at no extra cost.

## Can you pay less?

A few things move the bill:

- **Your insurer.** Extra contributions ranged from 2.18% to 4.39% in autumn 2026. At 3,000 euros of profit, that gap is about 66 euros a month. The downside is that benefits and service differ between insurers, so the rate isn't the only factor.
- **Sick pay.** The 0.6-point difference is 18 euros a month at 3,000 euros of profit.
- **Artists' fund.** If your work counts as artistic or publishing work, you may qualify for the <Term de="Künstlersozialkasse" />, which covers roughly half of your contributions, like an employer would. It suits writers, journalists, musicians and some designers. You need to earn at least 3,900 euros a year from that work, although lower income is allowed in your first three years, and you can't employ more than one person. The downside is that eligibility is checked closely and you report your expected income each year.

## Do you need a broker to sign up?

No. You can join a public insurer directly, online or by phone, at no cost. Every public insurer charges the same base rate, and only the extra contribution differs.

A broker can help with cover outside the public system, such as a private health policy or a private daily sick pay policy. Brokers earn a commission from the insurer when you sign up, which is worth knowing when you compare. A fee-based advisor, who charges you directly, has no commission at stake.

## What changes in January 2027?

These are the changes as of 25 November 2026. The care reform still needs parliament’s approval. We update this page when it is decided, and the date at the top shows when we last checked.

- **Income ceiling.** The ceiling rises from 5,812.50 to 6,375 euros a month. About 300 euros of that rise is a one-off extra increase fixed by a law passed in July 2026. The Bundesrat approved the ordinance with the new values on 20 November 2026. If your profit is above the current ceiling, your maximum bill rises.
- **Minimum.** Under the same ordinance, the reference figure rises from 3,955 to 4,130 euros a month. That lifts the minimum income your contribution is based on from 1,318.33 to 1,376.67 euros.
- **Extra contribution.** The health ministry has set the 2027 official average at 2.9%, unchanged. Each insurer sets its own rate, usually by the end of the year. If yours raises it, you have a special right to leave early: choose a new insurer by the end of the month in which the higher rate first applies. Since July 2026, insurers no longer have to write to you about a rise, so check your insurer's rate yourself.
- **Care insurance.** A care reform that the cabinet approved on 30 September 2026 would raise the total rate for people without children from 4.2% to 4.5%. It only applies if the Bundestag and Bundesrat pass it. The Bundestag held its first reading in November 2026.
- **Sick pay.** The new waiting period described above applies to late declarations.
