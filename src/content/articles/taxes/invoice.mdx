<ShortAnswer>
A German invoice is only valid for tax purposes if it shows the details listed in § 14 of the VAT Act (UStG): both parties' names and addresses, your tax number or VAT ID, the invoice date, a unique invoice number, a description, the service date, the net amount, the VAT rate and the VAT amount. Small businesses under the <Term de="Kleinunternehmerregelung" /> charge no VAT but must say why. Business clients in other EU countries pay no German VAT (reverse charge). From 2027 and 2028, invoices to German business clients must be e-invoices, and every invoice must be kept for eight years.
</ShortAnswer>

Leave out a required detail and your business client can lose the right to reclaim the VAT you charged. In practice, that means they ask you to reissue the invoice before they pay.

This guide is for freelancers (<Term de="Freiberufler" />), sole traders and small businesses invoicing from Germany. It walks through every mandatory field, the special rules for small businesses and foreign clients, and the e-invoicing deadlines that start on 1 January 2027.

## What a German invoice must include

Any invoice over €250 (gross) needs the nine details below, set out in § 14 (4) UStG. Two more apply only in special cases. It doesn't matter whether you send it on paper, as a PDF or as an e-invoice: the content rules are the same.

| # | Mandatory detail | German term | What counts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Your full name and address | Name und Anschrift des leistenden Unternehmers | Any address where you can be reached. A P.O. box is fine. |
| 2 | Your client's full name and address | Name und Anschrift des Leistungsempfängers | For companies, the legal name including the legal form (GmbH, UG, AG). |
| 3 | Your tax number or VAT ID | Steuernummer oder USt-IdNr. | One of the two is enough. |
| 4 | Invoice date | Ausstellungsdatum | The day you issue the invoice. |
| 5 | Unique invoice number | Rechnungsnummer | Must never repeat. Letters, several number ranges and gaps are allowed. |
| 6 | Quantity and description of what you delivered | Menge und Art der Lieferung / Art und Umfang der Leistung | Specific enough to identify the work. "Consulting" alone is too vague. |
| 7 | Date or period of delivery | Leistungsdatum / Leistungszeitraum | Required even if it matches the invoice date. The month is enough. |
| 8 | Net amount, split by VAT rate, plus any agreed discounts | Entgelt, aufgeschlüsselt nach Steuersätzen | A note such as "2% Skonto bei Zahlung bis …" covers early-payment discounts. |
| 9 | VAT rate and VAT amount, or a note on why no VAT applies | Steuersatz und Steuerbetrag / Hinweis auf Steuerbefreiung | Usually 19% (standard) or 7% (reduced). |
| 10 | Special case: the word "Gutschrift" when your client issues the invoice for you | Gutschrift | Only for self-billing arrangements. |
| 11 | Special case: a reverse-charge note when your client owes the VAT | Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers | Mainly for business clients in other EU countries. |

Run a company such as a GmbH or a registered merchant (e.K.)? Your invoices also count as business letters, so add your legal form, registry court and registration number, plus the managing directors for a GmbH.

## How to write your invoice, step by step

Work from top to bottom and you'll cover every mandatory field in eight steps.

1. **Put your details at the top.** Your name (or company name), full address and contact details go in the header.
2. **Add your client's details.** Use their full legal name and address, the way it appears in their imprint or contract.
3. **Add your tax number or VAT ID.** Many freelancers use their VAT ID (<Term de="USt-IdNr" />) so their personal tax number (<Term de="Steuernummer" />) stays private.
4. **Number and date the invoice.** Use a format that can never repeat, such as `2026-018`. Write dates the German way (`30.11.2026`) if your client is German.
5. **State when you did the work.** Add the delivery date or period, for example "Leistungszeitraum: November 2026". If it matches the invoice date, write "Leistungsdatum entspricht Rechnungsdatum".
6. **Describe each line item.** Give quantity, unit and unit price, and enough detail that an outsider can tell what you delivered.
7. **Show net, VAT and total separately.** Add up the net amounts, apply the VAT rate, then show the gross total. If no VAT applies, say why (see the special cases below).
8. **Add payment terms and send it.** Bank details and a due date aren't legally required, but they get you paid faster. Send invoices to business clients within six months of finishing the work.

## Special cases: no VAT, small amounts and foreign clients

Not every invoice shows 19% VAT. In these six situations, a specific note replaces or adds to the VAT line.

| Situation | German VAT? | Note on the invoice | What else changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| You're a Kleinunternehmer (§ 19 UStG) | No | "Gemäß § 19 UStG wird keine Umsatzsteuer berechnet." | Shorter list of required details |
| Invoice up to €250 gross | Yes | None | Gross total and VAT rate are enough |
| Therapy or other medical treatment (§ 4 Nr. 14 UStG) | No | "Umsatzsteuerfrei gemäß § 4 Nr. 14 UStG" | Otherwise the full list applies |
| Business client in another EU country | No | "Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers" (you can add "Reverse charge") | Both VAT IDs; issue by the 15th of the following month |
| Business client outside the EU | Usually no | Recommended: "Nicht im Inland steuerbare Leistung" | Keep proof that the client is a business |
| Private client, work on their property | Yes | A note that the client must keep the invoice for two years | Invoice is mandatory, within six months |

### Kleinunternehmer invoices

Since 1 January 2025, you qualify as a <Term de="Kleinunternehmer" /> if your turnover was no more than €25,000 last year and stays at or below €100,000 this year. Cross €100,000 mid-year and you charge VAT from that sale onward. The [small business VAT exemption](/taxes/kleinunternehmer) explains the limits and how to opt out.

Your invoice can be shorter (§ 34a UStDV): both names and addresses, your tax number or VAT ID, the invoice date, a description, the total and the § 19 note. An invoice number and service date are optional by law, but keep them so every invoice stays traceable. For English-speaking clients, add: "No VAT is charged under the small business exemption (§ 19 UStG)."

Never show VAT as a Kleinunternehmer. If you do, you owe that amount to the tax office anyway (§ 14c UStG).

### Invoices up to €250

For amounts up to €250 gross (<Term de="Kleinbetragsrechnung" />), five details are enough (§ 33 UStDV): your name and address, the invoice date, a description, the gross total and the VAT rate. This shortcut doesn't apply to reverse-charge invoices or to deliveries of goods to other EU countries.

### VAT-exempt services such as therapy

Healing treatments by doctors, psychotherapists, Heilpraktiker, physiotherapists and similar health professions are VAT-exempt under § 4 Nr. 14 UStG. Your invoice shows no VAT and names the exemption. Coaching, prevention courses or wellness offers without a therapeutic purpose are generally taxable, so invoice them separately.

### Business clients in other EU countries (reverse charge)

For most business-to-business services, VAT is due where the client is based. You charge no German VAT, and your client pays VAT in their own country.

Your invoice needs your VAT ID, your client's VAT ID and the reverse-charge note. Issue it by the 15th of the month after you finished the work. Report the sale in your VAT return and in the EC Sales List (<Term de="Zusammenfassende Meldung" />), due by the 25th of the month after each quarter.

### Clients outside the EU

Services to businesses outside the EU are usually not taxable in Germany. German law prescribes no specific note, but "Nicht im Inland steuerbare Leistung" (not taxable in Germany) is recommended. Keep proof that your client is a business, such as a certificate from their tax authority. Property-related services follow other rules, so check with a [tax advisor](/taxes/tax-advisor) first.

## E-invoicing: the 2027 and 2028 deadlines

From 1 January 2028, almost every invoice between two German businesses must be an e-invoice (<Term de="E-Rechnung" />). Larger businesses start a year earlier, on 1 January 2027.

An e-invoice is a structured, machine-readable file that follows the European standard EN 16931. In Germany, that usually means **XRechnung** (pure XML) or **ZUGFeRD** from version 2.0.1 (a PDF with the XML data embedded). The ZUGFeRD profiles MINIMUM and BASIC-WL don't count, because they lack mandatory fields. A normal PDF sent by email is *not* an e-invoice, even if it looks the same.

| From | Who | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Jan 2025 | Every business, including Kleinunternehmer | Must be able to **receive** e-invoices. An email inbox is enough. |
| 1 Jan 2027 | Businesses with more than €800,000 turnover in 2026 | Must **send** e-invoices to German business clients |
| 1 Jan 2027 | Businesses at or below €800,000 in 2026 | Can still send paper, or a PDF with the client's consent, until 31 Dec 2027 |
| 1 Jan 2028 | All businesses | Must send e-invoices to German business clients |

**Not affected:** invoices to private clients, invoices up to €250, VAT-exempt services such as therapy, and invoices to clients abroad. Kleinunternehmer never have to send e-invoices, but must still be able to receive them.

A guidance letter from the Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF) of 15 October 2026 adds two practical points:

- **Be specific.** The service description inside the structured data must make the type and scope of the work clear. A bare "Beratungsleistung" (consulting) may not be enough.
- **Embed attachments.** Timesheets or other supporting details must be embedded in the e-invoice file. A separate attachment in the same email doesn't count.

E-invoices issued until 31 December 2026 that meet the earlier BMF guidance are still accepted.

If you send a PDF when you're obliged to send an e-invoice, the invoice doesn't meet the rules: your client may refuse it, or lose their VAT deduction in an audit. Most invoicing software already exports XRechnung or ZUGFeRD.

## Invoice numbers, payment terms and corrections

### Invoice numbers

Each invoice number may only be used once. Beyond that, you have freedom: you can mix letters and digits, run several number ranges (for example one per client), and gaps are allowed.

Simple formats that work:

- `2026-001`, `2026-002` … (year plus counter)
- `RE-2611-018` (prefix, year, month, counter)
- `MUSTER-2026-04` (client code, year, counter)

If you restart your counter each year, keep the year in the number. Otherwise `001` would repeat.

### Payment terms and late payment

Payment terms aren't mandatory, but always add a due date, such as "Zahlbar innerhalb von 14 Tagen" (payable within 14 days). Want to reward fast payers? Offer an early-payment discount (<Term de="Skonto" />), for example "2% Skonto bei Zahlung innerhalb von 7 Tagen".

If a business client hasn't paid 30 days after the due date and after receiving your invoice, they're automatically in default (§ 286 BGB). Private clients are only in automatic default if your invoice tells them so. Once a client is in default, you can charge interest and fees:

| Client | Default interest (since 1 July 2026) | Extra |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business | 10.52% a year (base rate 1.52% + 9 points) | €40 flat fee per late invoice |
| Private | 6.52% a year (base rate 1.52% + 5 points) | None |

The base rate (Basiszinssatz) changes every 1 January and 1 July, so check it before you calculate interest. Start with a friendly payment reminder (Zahlungserinnerung), then a formal reminder (<Term de="Mahnung" />). A court payment order (Mahnbescheid) is the last step.

### Correcting or cancelling an invoice

Spotted a mistake? Don't edit and resend the same file. You have two options:

1. **Correct it.** Send a correction document (Rechnungskorrektur) that references the original invoice number and states the corrected details.
2. **Cancel and reissue.** Issue a cancellation invoice (<Term de="Stornorechnung" />) with the same amounts as negative values, referencing the original. Then send a new invoice with a new number.

Don't call a cancellation a <Term de="Gutschrift" />. In German tax law, that word means an invoice your client issues on your behalf, and it can cause confusion. If you showed too much VAT, you owe the full amount shown until you correct the invoice (§ 14c UStG).

### How long to keep invoices

Keep every invoice you send and receive for **eight years** (§ 14b UStG, shortened from ten years on 1 January 2025). The period starts at the end of the calendar year the invoice was issued. For e-invoices, keep the original XML file, not just a printout or PDF.

## 8 common invoicing mistakes (and how to fix them)

Most rejected invoices fail on the same handful of details. Check yours against this list before you send it.

| Mistake | Why it's a problem | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No service date | It's mandatory, even when it matches the invoice date | Add "Leistungsdatum entspricht Rechnungsdatum" or the month of work |
| Vague description | "Consulting" or "design work" doesn't identify the service | Name the project, deliverables and hours |
| Duplicate invoice number | Numbers must be unique | Include the year, or let software count for you |
| VAT shown as a Kleinunternehmer | You owe the VAT shown, even though you're exempt | Remove VAT and add the § 19 note; correct past invoices |
| German VAT charged to an EU business client | The client can't reclaim it, and you've filed it wrongly | Use reverse charge with both VAT IDs |
| Wrong VAT rate | Too much VAT is owed anyway; too little is a shortfall you pay | Check 19% vs. 7% vs. exempt before invoicing |
| Client's legal name incomplete | The client may not be able to deduct the VAT | Copy the name and legal form from their imprint or contract |
| PDF instead of an e-invoice once the rules apply to you | The invoice isn't compliant | Switch to XRechnung or ZUGFeRD before your deadline |

## When to switch to invoicing software

A template works well for a few invoices a month. By 1 January 2028 at the latest, though, every business that invoices German business clients (except Kleinunternehmer) needs a way to create XRechnung or ZUGFeRD files, and a word-processor template can't do that.

It's time to switch if any of these apply:

- You send more than five invoices a month.
- You invoice German business clients, so e-invoicing applies to you from 2027 or 2028.
- You have clients in several countries and need different VAT notes.
- You chase late payments by hand.
- Your tax advisor (<Term de="Steuerberater" />) asks for your records in DATEV format.

What to look for in an invoicing or accounting tool:

- Creates and receives XRechnung and ZUGFeRD e-invoices
- Ready-made layouts for Kleinunternehmer, reverse charge and VAT-exempt invoices
- Automatic, unique invoice numbers
- Payment reminders and overdue tracking
- A bank connection that matches payments to invoices
- A tamper-proof archive for the eight-year retention period
- DATEV export for your tax advisor
- An English interface, if German isn't your first language
