<ShortAnswer>
You are a <Term de="Freiberufler" /> if tax law names your kind of work as a freelance profession, or something closely similar, and you do the work yourself. Most other self-employed work, such as selling goods, is a <Term de="Gewerbe" /> (trade business). A Gewerbe adds a trade office registration, a compulsory chamber of commerce membership (<Term de="IHK" />) and possibly trade tax. You cannot simply choose: the tax office decides from what you actually do, and some jobs, such as software development, are gray cases.
</ShortAnswer>

## What is the difference between a Freiberufler and a Gewerbe?

Both are self-employment. Both pay income tax on their profit, which is revenue minus [deductible business expenses](/taxes/deductible-expenses-for-freelancers), and VAT works the same way for most people. The differences are in registration, one extra tax and one membership.

This is not a free choice. The <Term de="Finanzamt" /> (tax office) puts your work into one group, based on what you do. Picking the group that looks cheaper is not an option.

<ComparisonTable>

| | Freiberufler | Gewerbe |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | Tax office only | Trade office, then tax office |
| Trade tax | None | Yes, but none on the first €24,500 of profit |
| Chamber of commerce (IHK) | Not a member | Compulsory member; no fee at low profit |
| Bookkeeping | Simple profit calculation, always allowed | Simple until €800,000 revenue or €80,000 profit (since 2024) |
| Main downside | The tax office can question your status | More paperwork and the IHK |
| Best for | Work listed in tax law, such as translating, writing, teaching or engineering | Selling goods, trades and work not on the list |

</ComparisonTable>

Some regulated professions, such as lawyers and architects, have their own chambers instead of the IHK. The amounts in this table, and the other yearly thresholds, are collected on our [freelancer numbers](/freelancer-numbers) page.

## Who counts as a Freiberufler?

Section 18 of the Income Tax Act (EStG) defines freelance work. It covers two groups:

- **Listed professions** (<Term de="Katalogberufe" />): doctors, lawyers, architects, engineers, tax advisors, translators, interpreters, journalists and a few others.
- **Freelance activities:** independent scientific, artistic, writing, teaching and educating work.

A job that is not on the list can still count if it is similar to a listed job, in both the training it needs and the work itself.

Two conditions apply to everyone. You work independently, not as an employee. And you lead the work and do its core yourself, even if staff help you.

In practice, the test comes down to three questions:

1. Is your work on the list, or similar to a job on it?
2. Do you do the core work yourself and independently?
3. Does all your income come from work covered by questions 1 and 2?

If you can answer yes to all three, your work is likely freelance. A "no" points toward a Gewerbe or a mixed case.

## Where do common freelance jobs land?

This is a rough guide. The tax office looks at what you actually do, not at your job title.

Usually treated as Freiberufler:

- Translators and interpreters
- Journalists, authors and other writers
- Engineers and architects with the matching qualification
- Artists and musicians
- Teachers and tutors, including language teachers

Usually treated as Gewerbe:

- Selling goods, including online shops and dropshipping
- Craft and trade work
- Most online marketing, such as SEO and social media management

Gray cases, where the details decide:

- Software development
- IT and business consulting
- Graphic design and photography
- Coaching and copywriting

In gray cases, the question is usually whether the work is creative, scientific or comparable to engineering, or whether it is closer to delivering a standard service to a client's specification.

Software development shows how this works. The Federal Fiscal Court (<Term de="BFH" />) has ruled that a developer can do work similar to an engineer's, and so count as a Freiberufler, whether they write system software or business applications. Two things must come together: knowledge comparable to a computer science degree, either through the degree itself or provable self-taught knowledge, and work that actually involves designing and developing. Implementing someone else's specification, configuring systems or selling software and licenses points to a Gewerbe.

## Who decides, and can the answer change?

The tax office decides. You send it a questionnaire when you start (see below), describe your work, and it classifies you. The trade office has its own test, so the two offices can reach different answers.

Describe what you actually do, in specific terms: what you deliver, to whom, and what training the work needs.

<Callout title="The tax office can change your status later">
If it decides your work is a Gewerbe, you may owe trade tax for earlier years whose assessments can still be changed. Normally that reaches back four years from the end of the year you filed each return, and longer if tax was evaded. You would also need to register as a Gewerbe, and you become an IHK member from the day the trade activity started, so the IHK can charge fees for those years too, unless your profit was small enough to be exempt.
</Callout>

If your work is a gray case, or you mix freelance and trade income, a [tax advisor](/taxes/tax-advisor) (<Term de="Steuerberater" />) can assess it and tell you how the tax office is likely to see it. That is worth doing before you start, not after.

## What if you do both freelance and trade work?

Some people do both. A translator might also sell products online, for example.

If you work alone, the tax office treats the two activities separately whenever they can be separated in practice, even if they are related. Separate records, invoices and bank accounts make that easy to show. Only if the two depend on each other so closely that you deliver them as one service, such as installing software together with hardware you sell, does the tax office look at the whole. Then the activity that shapes your business decides, and everything can become a Gewerbe.

If you run the business with partners, for example in a GbR (a partnership under civil law), a stricter rule applies. A small amount of trade income can turn all of the partnership's income into trade income. This is called the <Term de="Abfärbetheorie" /> (section 15 EStG). The Federal Fiscal Court has set a limit for minor amounts: the partnership's income stays freelance if its trade revenue, after VAT, is no more than 3 percent of its total revenue and no more than €24,500 a year (ruling of 27 August 2014, VIII R 6/12). Both conditions must be met.

## What does a Gewerbe cost?

Trade tax (<Term de="Gewerbesteuer" />) goes to your city. A sole proprietor pays none on the first €24,500 of trade profit a year. Above that, the tax is 3.5 percent of the excess, multiplied by your city's rate (<Term de="Hebesatz" />).

Here is a simplified example. It assumes a profit of €60,000 in Berlin, where the rate has been 410 percent since 1999.

- Profit above the allowance: €35,500
- 3.5 percent of that: €1,242.50
- Multiplied by 410 percent: about €5,094 in trade tax

Sole proprietors can then credit up to four times €1,242.50, which is €4,970, against their income tax (§ 35 EStG). In this example, about €124 is left. The credit cannot be higher than the income tax you owe on that profit. The real calculation also adds some costs back to your profit, so your result can differ.

The other costs are smaller but regular:

- **IHK fee.** Every Gewerbe is a compulsory member of the local IHK. Sole proprietors with a yearly trade profit up to €5,200 pay nothing. New business owners who were not self-employed in the previous five years pay nothing in the year they start and the year after, and only the basic fee in the third and fourth year, as long as profit stays at or below €25,000. Above these limits, the fee depends on your IHK and your profit.
- **A trade tax return** in addition to your income tax return, once your trade profit is above €24,500 a year. Below that, you only file one if the tax office asks for it or you have a recorded trade loss to carry forward.
- **Possible double-entry accounting.** Since 2024, the tax office can require it if revenue passes €800,000 or profit passes €80,000 in a year. A Freiberufler can always use the simple profit calculation (<Term de="Einnahmenüberschussrechnung" />), and so can a Gewerbe below those limits. An accounting app such as <AffiliateLink slug="accountable">Accountable</AffiliateLink> can prepare it from your receipts and bank transactions.

## How do you register?

<Callout title="You have one month">
Both groups must send the <Term de="Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung" /> (tax registration questionnaire) to the tax office within one month of starting work. Since 2021 it must be filed online through <Term de="ELSTER" />, the tax office's portal. Creating an ELSTER account can take a few days or longer, so do that first.
</Callout>

As a Freiberufler:

1. Create an ELSTER account if you do not have one.
2. File the questionnaire in ELSTER within one month of starting.
3. The tax office sends you your tax number.

As a Gewerbe:

1. Register at the trade office (<Term de="Gewerbeamt" />) of your city or district when you start. The fee depends on the city and is usually between about €15 and €65; in Berlin it is €15 online. The office informs the tax office, the IHK and other bodies.
2. File the questionnaire in ELSTER yourself within one month. The trade registration does not replace it.
3. Expect a letter from your IHK about membership and fees.

The questionnaire is explained step by step in [Getting your tax number: the tax registration questionnaire in English](/start/tax-registration-questionnaire). The whole process is in [How to become a freelancer in Germany: step by step](/start/become-a-freelancer).

## Does it matter for your visa?

If you need a residence permit to work for yourself, the immigration office (<Term de="Ausländerbehörde" />) assesses freelance and trade work under separate rules in section 21 of the Residence Act:

- **Trade work** (paragraph 1) needs an economic interest or a regional need, expected positive effects on the economy, and secured financing. Bodies such as the IHK give their opinion on your business plan.
- **Freelance work** (paragraph 5) can be allowed without those three conditions, as an exception. Any professional license your work needs, for example as a doctor, must already be granted or promised.

The English word "freelancer" does not automatically mean Freiberufler in this sense, so check which rules apply to your type of work before you register.
