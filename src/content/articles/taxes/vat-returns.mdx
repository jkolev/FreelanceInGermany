<ShortAnswer>
If you charge German VAT, you must report it to the tax office (<Term de="Finanzamt" />) in an advance VAT return, the <Term de="Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung" />, and pay what you owe. You file it online in <Term de="ELSTER" />, monthly or quarterly, by the 10th day after the period ends. What you pay is the VAT you charged clients minus the VAT you paid on business costs. You don't file one if you use the small business exemption or your work is VAT-exempt, with a few exceptions covered below.
</ShortAnswer>

## What is the advance VAT return?

VAT is called <Term de="Umsatzsteuer" /> in German. When you invoice a client with VAT, you collect that tax for the state. It is not your income.

The advance VAT return tells the tax office how much VAT you collected and how much you can deduct. The deduction is called <Term de="Vorsteuer" />. It is the VAT other businesses charged you on things you bought for work, such as a laptop or software. You need an invoice that shows the VAT to deduct it.

Each period, you subtract your Vorsteuer from the VAT you charged and pay the difference. If your Vorsteuer is higher, the tax office refunds the difference.

Here is an example. In one quarter you invoice €5,000 net and charge 19% VAT, which is €950. You also buy equipment for €800 net, with €152 of VAT. You pay €950 minus €152, which is €798.

Most freelance services carry 19% VAT. The reduced rate of 7% applies when you grant usage rights under copyright, for example as an author, illustrator or composer whose work is published (§ 12 (2) no. 7c UStG). What counts is the work, not the job title: a literary translator whose translation is published charges 7%, while a translator of business documents charges 19%. Journalists may apply 7% to all their work, unless they only pass on data such as share prices or sports results. Software development is generally taxed at 19%.

The word "advance" matters. These payments are prepayments. After the year ends, you also file an annual VAT return (<Term de="Umsatzsteuererklärung" />), which settles the final figure. It is due on 31 July of the following year if you file it yourself, and at the end of February of the second following year if a tax advisor files it for you.

VAT prepayments are separate from income tax prepayments. For how VAT must appear on your invoices, see [How to write a German invoice](/taxes/invoice). Which purchases count as business costs is covered in [Deductible expenses for freelancers](/taxes/deductible-expenses-for-freelancers).

## Do you have to file one?

It depends on whether you charge VAT.

- **If you charge VAT,** you file advance returns. This covers most freelancers.
- **If you use the small business exemption** (<Term de="Kleinunternehmerregelung" />), you charge no VAT and file no VAT returns at all, neither advance nor annual. The exception: if you owe VAT yourself on something you bought, mainly services from a business abroad (reverse charge), you file an advance return for that period (§ 18 (4a) UStG). See [The small business VAT exemption](/taxes/kleinunternehmer).
- **If your work is VAT-exempt,** you charge no VAT. Some activities, such as certain medical and teaching work, are exempt under § 4 UStG. You generally can't deduct Vorsteuer either.

## How often do you file, and when is it due?

The tax office decides how often you file, based on the VAT you owed in the previous year. Since 2025, the limits are:

- More than €9,000: monthly returns
- Up to €9,000: quarterly returns
- Up to €2,000: the tax office can release you from advance returns, and you file only the annual one

Look for your filing period in the letter that confirms your tax number (<Term de="Steuernummer" />). If you can't find it, ask your tax office.

<Callout title="New businesses: monthly filing returns in 2027">
In the year you start and the following year, there is no previous year to go by. Until the end of 2026, the tax office sets your period from the VAT you expect, so many new freelancers file quarterly. That exception was written into the law only for 2021 to 2026. Unless it is extended, from 1 January 2027 new businesses must file monthly in their first and second calendar year, however small the amount. If you start in 2026, 2027 counts as your second year. New businesses cannot be released from advance returns in these two years.
</Callout>

{/* LINK: once published, add: "Your expected VAT is one of the questions in [the tax registration questionnaire](/start/tax-registration-questionnaire)." */}

Both the return and the payment are due on the 10th day after the period ends. For quarterly filers:

| Quarter | Covers | Due |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | January to March | 10 April |
| 2 | April to June | 10 July |
| 3 | July to September | 10 October |
| 4 | October to December | 10 January |

Monthly filers file by the 10th of the following month. The January return is due on 10 February.

You can ask for a one-month extension, called <Term de="Dauerfristverlängerung" />, through ELSTER. Apply by the date your first return under it would be due. Every due date then moves back a month, so the first-quarter return is due on 10 May. The extension stays in place until you or the tax office end it.

Monthly filers who use it pay a deposit each year, the <Term de="Sondervorauszahlung" />, of one eleventh of the previous year's VAT prepayments, by 10 February. It is credited back against the December return. Quarterly filers pay no deposit.

<Callout title="Filing and paying are both due on the 10th">
If the 10th falls on a weekend or public holiday, the deadline moves to the next working day. Filing late can bring a late-filing surcharge (<Term de="Verspätungszuschlag" />): for advance returns, the tax office decides whether to charge one and how much, based on how late and how often you are, up to €25,000. Paying late adds a late-payment surcharge (<Term de="Säumniszuschlag" />) of 1% of the unpaid amount, rounded down to the nearest €50, for each month started. If a bank transfer arrives up to three days late, no surcharge is charged, but this grace period doesn't cover cash or cheques, and there is none for filing.
</Callout>

## How do you file it?

You file electronically through ELSTER, the free online portal of the German tax authorities. The tax office accepts paper only in hardship cases.

<Callout title="Register with ELSTER before your first due date">
After you register, ELSTER sends an activation code by post, which usually arrives within about two weeks. If you have a German ID card with the online function switched on, you can verify your identity online instead and skip the letter.
</Callout>

1. Register at elster.de and activate your account.
2. Log in and open the form called Umsatzsteuer-Voranmeldung. Choose the year and the period.
3. Enter your net revenue by VAT rate and your Vorsteuer. ELSTER calculates the VAT you owe.
4. Check the figures, send the return and keep the confirmation ELSTER shows.
5. Pay the amount to your tax office by the due date. You can also give the tax office a direct debit mandate (SEPA-Lastschriftmandat) so it collects the amount itself.

## Which fields do you fill in?

The fields on the form have numbers called <Term de="Kennzahl" /> (Kz). Most freelancers use only a few:

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Kz 81 | Net revenue taxed at 19%, in whole euros |
| Kz 86 | Net revenue taxed at 7%, in whole euros |
| Kz 66 | Vorsteuer from invoices of other businesses |
| Kz 21 | Net revenue from services to business clients in other EU countries |
| Kz 45 | Other revenue not taxable in Germany, for example services to business clients outside the EU |
| Kz 46 and 47 | Services you bought from businesses abroad (reverse charge): the net amount and the VAT you owe on it. You deduct the same VAT again in Kz 67. |
| Kz 83 | The result: the amount you owe or your refund. ELSTER fills it in, and it is never left empty. |

## When is VAT due: when you invoice or when you get paid?

By default, VAT is due for the period in which you do the work, even if the client hasn't paid. This is <Term de="Sollversteuerung" />. You can end up paying VAT on money you haven't received yet.

Freelancers (<Term de="Freiberufler" />) can ask the tax office to report VAT only when the client pays. This is <Term de="Istversteuerung" />, and freelancers can use it whatever their turnover. Other self-employed people can use it if their turnover last year was no more than €800,000. You can request it in the registration questionnaire or later with an informal application to the tax office.

Istversteuerung tends to suit people whose clients pay slowly. The trade-off is that you must track the payment date of every invoice.

## What if you invoice clients abroad?

Services to business clients outside Germany are generally taxed where the client is based (§ 3a (2) UStG), so you don't charge German VAT. You still report them on the return:

- **Business clients in other EU countries:** enter the net amount in Kz 21. Your invoice needs your VAT ID and your client's VAT ID, plus a reverse-charge note. You get your VAT ID (<Term de="Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer" />) from the Federal Central Tax Office (BZSt). You also file a separate quarterly report, the <Term de="Zusammenfassende Meldung" />, by the 25th day after each quarter. The one-month extension does not apply to it.
- **Business clients outside the EU:** enter the net amount in Kz 45.
- **Private clients abroad:** these are often taxed in Germany, so you charge German VAT. Digital services to private clients in other EU countries follow special rules.

The rules differ by type of service and by client. This is a point where a tax advisor can help. See [Do you need a tax advisor?](/taxes/tax-advisor)
