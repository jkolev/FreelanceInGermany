<ShortAnswer>
If you start working for yourself in Germany, you must tell your <Term de="Finanzamt" /> (tax office) within one month. You do this with the <Term de="Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung" /> (tax registration questionnaire), which you submit online through <Term de="ELSTER" />. The tax office reviews your answers and posts your <Term de="Steuernummer" /> (tax number) to you. The form is in German, and your profit estimates and VAT choices affect what you pay later.
</ShortAnswer>

## Do you have to fill it in?

Yes. Anyone who starts working as a freelancer, or opens a trade business, must report it to the tax office within one month of starting. The rule is in § 138 of the German Fiscal Code. The questionnaire is how you report: it must be sent electronically, and the tax office accepts paper only on request, to avoid hardship.

Freelancers (<Term de="Freiberufler" />) report straight to the tax office. They do not register with the trade office (<Term de="Gewerbeamt" />). If your work counts as a trade (<Term de="Gewerbe" />), you register with the trade office first and still submit the questionnaire within the month.

The tax office decides which of the two applies, based on what you write in the form. [Freiberufler vs. Gewerbe: which one are you?](/start/freiberufler-vs-gewerbe) explains how that decision works. Freelancers and sole traders use the version for sole proprietors (<Term de="Einzelunternehmen" />). Partnerships and companies have their own versions.

<Callout title="Tax registration is not permission to work">
The tax office and the immigration office are separate. Your residence permit decides whether you may work for yourself.{/* LINK: once published, add: "[The freelance visa](/start/freelance-visa) explains how permits and self-employment fit together." */}
</Callout>

## What do you need before you start?

Have these ready before you open the form:

- **Your tax ID** (<Term de="Steueridentifikationsnummer" />), an 11-digit personal number. The Federal Central Tax Office (<Term de="Bundeszentralamt für Steuern" />, BZSt) posts it to you after you first register your address in Germany.
- **Bank details**, because the form asks for them so the tax office can pay you refunds.
- **A short description of your work in German**, and the date you start.
- **An ELSTER account**.

ELSTER registration is free and happens once. Part of your access data arrives by email and part by post, so the Bavarian tax authority suggests registering shortly before you start.

If you have no tax ID or German tax number yet, you can register with only an email address. That account can submit the questionnaire and nothing else. You upgrade it after you receive your tax number, which can take up to two weeks.

Since 2024, you can switch ELSTER's menus and navigation to English (in the menu at the top right). The translation is machine-made, and the questionnaire's fields are still largely in German, so this guide gives the English meaning of each part.

## How do you submit it?

1. Go to elster.de and create your account. The link is called **Benutzerkonto erstellen**.
2. Log in to **Mein ELSTER**.
3. Open **Formulare & Leistungen**, then **Alle Formulare**.
4. Select the **Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung** for sole proprietors.
5. Enter your tax office. For freelancers this is normally the office for the area where you live. You can look yours up with the [BZSt tax office finder](https://www.bzst.de/DE/Service/Behoerdenwegweiser/Finanzamtsuche/finanzamtsuche.html).
6. Fill in the sections below, check your answers and submit.

## What does the questionnaire ask?

The form has many sections, and a one-person freelance business answers only some of them. The names below are as they appear on the form. Line numbers differ between versions, so this guide does not use them.

### Your activity and start date

In **Art der Tätigkeit** (type of activity), describe in German what you do for clients. The tax office uses this text to decide whether your work is freelance or a trade, so be specific. If your work is not on the list of freelance professions in § 18 of the Income Tax Act, the office checks whether it is similar to one.

**Beginn der Tätigkeit** (start of activity) is the date you start or started. Preparations count: if you bought equipment or rented an office for the business before your first client, the start can be that earlier date. If you are already working, enter your real start date, even if it lies in the past.

### Expected profit and turnover

The form asks for the profit (<Term de="Gewinn" />) you expect in the year you start and in the following year. Profit is what is left after business costs. It is different from turnover (<Term de="Umsatz" />), which is the total you invoice. You also enter other income, such as a salary, and your spouse's income if you are married.

The tax office uses these figures to set your income tax prepayments (<Term de="Steuervorauszahlung" />). Higher estimates lead to higher prepayments, and lower ones can mean a larger bill later. Prepayments can be changed afterward: send the tax office an informal request with an updated profit estimate, for example through ELSTER. See [Tax prepayments: why they arrive and how to lower them](/taxes/tax-prepayments).

### How you calculate profit

Under **Gewinnermittlung** (profit calculation) you choose the method. Freelancers may use the simple income-surplus method (<Term de="Einnahmenüberschussrechnung" />, EÜR), which counts money in and money out. Double-entry bookkeeping is the other main option. If you choose the EÜR, an accounting app such as <AffiliateLink slug="accountable">Accountable</AffiliateLink> can keep track of your income and expenses as you go and prepare the figures at the end of the year.

### VAT questions

The VAT (<Term de="Umsatzsteuer" />) part asks for your expected turnover in the year you start and the next year. It then asks whether you want to use the small business scheme (<Term de="Kleinunternehmerregelung" />). Since 2025 you can use it if your turnover last year was at most €25,000 and stays at or under €100,000 this year, both counted net of VAT. In the year you start, a fixed limit of €25,000 applies to that year instead. If you pass a limit during the year, the exemption ends with the sale that takes you over it, not at the end of the year.

With the scheme, you charge no VAT on invoices and cannot reclaim VAT on business purchases. [The small business VAT exemption](/taxes/kleinunternehmer) explains the trade-offs.

<Callout title="Giving up the small business scheme is hard to undo">
If you tell the tax office you want to charge VAT even though you qualify, that choice binds you for at least five calendar years (§ 19 Abs. 3 UStG).
</Callout>

If you charge VAT, the form also asks whether you pay it for the period in which you do the work (<Term de="Sollversteuerung" />, the default) or only on money you have received (<Term de="Istversteuerung" />). With the second, you owe VAT only after a client pays. A Freiberufler can apply for it whatever their turnover.

The form also asks how much VAT you expect to owe, which the tax office uses to set how often you file advance VAT returns. From 2027, new businesses must file them monthly in the year they start and the following year, unless that rule is extended again. [VAT returns explained](/taxes/vat-returns) has the details.

You need a VAT ID (<Term de="Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer" />), for example, to invoice business clients in other EU countries. You can request it on the form, and the BZSt issues it after the tax office registers you for VAT. You can also apply to the BZSt directly later.

### The other parts

The personal details part asks for your religion, because church tax (<Term de="Kirchensteuer" />) applies to members of some religious communities. Several other parts concern employees, entries in the commercial register, construction work or a company group. They usually do not apply to a one-person freelance business.

## What if you already have a tax number from filing as an employee?

The form asks whether you were registered with a tax office for income tax in the last three years, and the same for your spouse if you are married. If you were, you enter that tax office and your old tax number. If you have no tax number yet, you leave that field empty, and your tax ID goes in the personal details.

After you submit, the tax office decides how to continue. You may receive a new tax number for your freelance work, or the tax office may keep using your existing one. The letter you receive tells you which number to use from then on, including on your invoices.

## What happens after you submit?

The tax office reviews your answers and may ask follow-up questions or request documents. It assigns your tax number only once the form and any requested documents have arrived. The number comes by post.

There is no official processing time, and waiting times differ between tax offices. Freelancers commonly report two to six weeks, sometimes longer in big cities. A complete form with a clear description of your work avoids follow-up questions, which cause most delays.

Your invoices must show your tax number, or your VAT ID if you have one (§ 14 UStG). That is why your first invoice usually waits for the letter. [How to write a German invoice](/taxes/invoice) covers the rest.

If you registered with an email-only ELSTER account, upgrade it once the number arrives. Until then you cannot use ELSTER for VAT returns. Your VAT ID, if you asked for one, arrives separately from the BZSt.

The BZSt has also been issuing a newer business ID (<Term de="Wirtschafts-Identifikationsnummer" />) in stages since November 2024, and will continue into 2027. For now it does not replace anything: your tax number stays valid, and on invoices you still use your tax number or VAT ID.

## Can someone else fill it in for you?

Yes. A tax advisor (<Term de="Steuerberater" />) can submit the questionnaire for you and can answer questions about the choices above. Ask what they charge before you agree. Commercial services that submit it for you also exist.
